The Corps needs a way to file a species. A medic needs to know what a body is made of before treating one, a quartermaster needs it before fitting a uniform shell, and a life-support officer needs it before deciding what a compartment must contain. So there is a classification, it is used daily, and **it is not a Linnaean taxonomy** — because it cannot be.

## The Problem Nobody Can Solve

Earth's taxonomy is a **statement about ancestry**. Two organisms share a phylum because they share an ancestor that had the relevant body plan, and the whole structure is a family tree with the ranks drawn across it. That is what makes it powerful: the classification predicts things it was not built from, because descent leaves traces everywhere.

Across biospheres there is no tree. The [Veyr](/star-rangers/lore/veyr-basaltborn/) and the [Chthonari](/star-rangers/lore/chthonari/) share no ancestor because there is no ancestor to share — their lineages began separately, on separate worlds, from separate chemistry. Two species that look alike are **convergent design or coincidence, never branches of one line**, which is the same rule the record already applies to [membranes](/star-rangers/lore/concordant-membranes/) and for the same reason.

So a cross-species phylum would be a claim about kinship where no kinship exists. The Survey Corps declined to make one, and split the job in two instead.

## It Goes Deeper Than Ancestry

The absence of a shared ancestor is the easy half. The hard half is that **there is no shared substrate to be ancestral in.**

Nothing in this record obliges another biosphere to use DNA, or nucleic acids at all, or a discrete particulate heredity of any kind. Nothing obliges it to be built of cells. Nothing obliges the division between animal and plant that Solar System biology treats as a fact about life rather than a fact about *one* line of it, and **no other people in this record is a metazoan** — not distantly, not in a modified form, not at all, because metazoa are a clade and a clade is a family, and none of these peoples is in the family.

**And no, it was not seeded.** The obvious objection to all of this is panspermia — the idea that life, or its chemistry, drifted between worlds and gave everyone a common start after all. The record refuses it, on grounds it considers unremarkable: a long fragile information-bearing polymer would have to survive hard vacuum, unfiltered ultraviolet, cosmic radiation and interstellar timescales, then arrive at a competent velocity and pass through an atmosphere without being cooked, and do all of that often enough to matter. **DNA drifting through space is not a mechanism; it is a wish for a tidier galaxy.** Nothing in the survey record supports it, and the alternative it is invoked to avoid — that life started separately, several times, out of whatever each world had to hand — is both simpler and what the evidence looks like.

This is why the Corps' vocabulary has to be read carefully. When a Survey entry says a Verdani *symbiont*, a Chthonari *exoskeleton* or a Prismeri *metamorphosis*, it is using an Earth word for a functionally similar thing, on the record's [standing principle](/star-rangers/lore/timekeeping-and-the-common-record/) that the original is the document and the conversion is the courtesy. The word marks a resemblance in what the thing *does*. It asserts nothing about what it is made of or how it works, and a medic who reads *exoskeleton* and reaches for chitin chemistry has made exactly the error the whole scheme exists to prevent.

**The consequence for the second rank is uncomfortable and the page states it rather than hiding it.** Within one biosphere there is genuine continuity between generations — that much is observed, not assumed, or there would be no species to classify. But *descent*, *inheritance* and *lineage* are Solar System words for it, and the mechanism differs by world and in several cases is not fully characterised. Verdance's kinbed bonding passes a partner rather than a gene, and is treated by the Verdani as more binding than parentage; that is not a quaint local custom sitting on top of a normal heredity, it may simply be what heredity is there.

So the lineage rank is **also a translation**, and a less secure one than it looks. The Corps uses it because a medic treating a Chthonari benefits enormously from knowing which other Undersong organisms that Chthonari resembles, and because within a world the predictions hold. It is a working tool, not a claim about how life reproduces in general, and the Archive would rather say so than be caught assuming.

## Frames: Structure, and No Claim of Ancestry

A **frame** answers one question — *how is this body held up?* — and answers it functionally. It is an engineering classification wearing biological clothes, it is honest about being one, and it deliberately carries **no implication whatever of relatedness**.

| Frame | Structural strategy | Members |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Trabeculata** | Open lattice. Load carried through many slender braced members with no solid trunk | [Veyr Basaltborn](/star-rangers/lore/veyr-basaltborn/) |
| **Cataphracta** | Solid external shell, dense and sealed | [Chthonari](/star-rangers/lore/chthonari/); vessik; [Serephine Dunekin](/star-rangers/lore/serephine-dunekin/) |
| **Hydrostata** | Fluid pressure. No rigid skeleton at all | [Pelagene Littoral](/star-rangers/lore/pelagene-littoral/); [Cerebraun](/star-rangers/lore/cerebraun/) |
| **Turgorida** | Turgor and fibre, in the manner of a plant | [Verdani](/star-rangers/lore/verdani/) |
| **Endoskelia** | Internal skeleton | Humans; [Krenyi](/star-rangers/lore/krenyi/); [Prismeri](/star-rangers/lore/prismeri/) |

Three things about that table are worth saying aloud, because trainees get all three wrong.

**Endoskelia contains humans and Krenyi, and they are not related.** This is the example the Academy uses, because it teaches the whole scheme in one line. Two peoples of independent origin arrived at an internal skeleton, two arms and an upright stance, and the frame records the resemblance while asserting nothing about where it came from. A frame is a description, not a genealogy. *(It is also, per [the standing refusal](/star-rangers/lore/what-the-record-refuses/), a very small club: those are the only two sapient humanoids in the record.)*

**Cataphracta contains the Chthonari and the Serephine**, whose worlds could hardly be less alike — negligible-gravity asteroid burrows against a thin-aired world of lethal thermal swing — and whose shells solve entirely different problems. Same answer, different question. The frame is not embarrassed by this.

**And the frame does not capture what a species is like.** A Serephine's defining trait is that the sealed shell *opens twice a day*; a Chthonari's is that the shell carries vibration-sensing organs along every limb. Both are Cataphracta. Anyone who thinks they now know something about either has misread the table.

## Lineages: Ancestry, and Never More Than One World

The second rank is the one that does what a genus does, and it works properly — because **within a single biosphere, common descent is real.** A lineage groups a species with its actual relatives: the other organisms on its own world that share its ancestry, at roughly the depth Earth's usage means by a genus.

Lineages are therefore always **local**, always **genuinely phylogenetic**, and can never be compared across worlds. A Chthonari lineage and a Verdani lineage are not two of the same kind of thing; they are two claims about two unrelated family trees, filed in the same drawer for convenience.

Where a people has its own system, the Corps uses theirs and says so. Where it does not, Survey applies a provisional coinage and marks it provisional, on the same principle that governs [translated dates](/star-rangers/lore/timekeeping-and-the-common-record/): the original is the document, the conversion is the courtesy.

The clearest local case is also the most awkward. **Verdani taxonomy classifies by symbiont, not by host.** A Verdani's [kinstrain](/star-rangers/lore/verdani/) — Farrowkin, Duskkin, Rootkin — names the thallenwort lineage they were bonded into, and it is the category that matters to them, socially, medically and legally. It cuts straight across host ancestry, and it is not a mistake or a folk system awaiting correction: for a people whose metabolism runs on the partnership, the partner's lineage genuinely is the more predictive fact. Survey files Verdani twice, and the two filings disagree, and both are right.

## The Krenyi Decline to Be Filed

Predictably, and on their own terms. A people whose central practice is [refusing to be placed](/star-rangers/lore/krenyi/) — who answer the material questions without hesitation and decline the homeworld question because *where are you from*, asked as a way to explain them, makes a person the product of a place — are not going to accept a lineage.

They do not object to the frame. Endoskelia is a statement about how they stand up, it is accurate, and a Krenyi will confirm it as readily as they confirm that they are long-lived. What they decline is the second rank, and the Archive's entry for Krenyi lineage is **empty on purpose**, in the same way and for the same reason as their origin entry.

Survey has never pressed it. The medical value of the frame is real; the lineage would have bought a filing convenience, and buying that at the cost of the one thing a people has asked of the record is a poor trade that nobody has seriously proposed twice.

## What It Is Actually For

Not science. **Medicine, life support and fitting.**

A frame tells a medic what will happen if pressure changes, where load goes when a body is injured, and whether a compound will cross an integument. It tells a quartermaster that a shell must be fitted per body plan rather than cut to a pattern — the reason the [Ranger uniform standard](/star-rangers/lore/rank-insignia-and-uniform/) is modular in the first place. It tells a life-support officer what a compartment must contain, and it is why a Pelagene's hydration cycles and a Serephine's respiratory burden appear on a duty roster rather than in a footnote.

Everything the scheme deliberately cannot do — trace descent, predict relatedness, rank one people against another — is something the Corps has no operational use for anyway. **It is a classification built by people who needed to keep everyone alive, not by people who wanted a tree.**
