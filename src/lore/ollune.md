Ask an Ollune why they cried out, and the question does not translate. Crying out is free. It costs nothing, binds nothing, and commits no one to anything, and an Ollune who felt the air go wrong and said so has not made a claim, laid an accusation, or accepted a duty. They have only made a noise that everyone in the band can hear. To an Ollune, asking why someone raised an alarm is like asking why they exhaled.

The **Ollune** — the word is their own, and renders roughly as *the held* — are a sapient people native to the upper atmosphere of [Cirrane](/star-rangers/lore/planets/cirrane/), a gas giant in the Vantine System and the only [Federation of Sentient Beings](/star-rangers/lore/federation-of-sentient-beings/) member world with no surface at all. They have no ground, no horizon, no fire, no metal, and no possibility of having built a vessel that could leave. Every distinctive thing about them follows from the same physical fact: an Ollune body stays up only for as long as it keeps paying to.

## Buoyancy as Metabolism

An Ollune is large, soft-bodied, and mostly envelope — a translucent gas bladder several metres across, trailing a fringe of manipulating tendrils and filter-vanes beneath it. In Cirrane's hydrogen-helium atmosphere there is no lighter gas available to fill it with, hydrogen being already the lightest thing there is. So an Ollune does not float on a lighter gas. It floats on a *warmer* one, holding its envelope above ambient temperature by continuous metabolic effort, every hour it is alive.

This is the fact that organises the species. Lift is not a property an Ollune has; it is a bill an Ollune pays. Illness, injury, starvation and sleep all reduce the payment, and reduced payment means descent — into higher pressure, higher temperature, and eventually into conditions no body survives. An Ollune does not lie down to rest. There is nothing to lie down on. An Ollune who stops attending to their own buoyancy sinks, and the Ollune language has no separate words for falling asleep and going under.

Which is why they sleep in **holds**: tethered clusters of eight to thirty, physically linked at the tendril, in which the waking carry the sinking. Every Ollune alive has been held unconscious by others, nightly, since infancy, and expects to be again tonight. Federation xenosociologists reaching for a human comparison usually settle on none, and say instead what the Ollune say — that among a people whose word for themselves means *the held*, trust is not a sentiment about someone. It is a load being borne, by named individuals, right now.

## Mass Is a Tax

Anything an Ollune carries must be lifted for as long as they own it. There is no setting it down, no storehouse, no floor. A possession is a permanent metabolic charge against the body holding it, and the charge is paid hourly, forever, by that body alone.

An economy cannot become acquisitive under that constraint, and Ollune economics never did. What an Ollune holds is what an Ollune is currently using; what an Ollune has instead of wealth is standing — a claim on the lift of others, in a hold that will carry them through illness, injury, and old age. Their approximate word for a rich individual translates as *heavy*, and is not flattering. Federation economists have written a good deal about this, most of it noting that the Ollune did not choose an ethic of non-accumulation over any other, and would not necessarily have chosen it. Cirrane declined to offer the alternative.

## A People Without Fire

Cirrane's atmosphere carries no free oxygen. Nothing on Cirrane burns, has ever burned, or can be made to burn, and no Ollune has ever seen a flame except through a Federation viewport. With no fire there is no smelting; with no surface there are no ore bodies to smelt, Cirrane's metals having settled toward its core long before there was anyone to want them.

So Ollune technology is entirely biochemical. Where another civilisation would forge a thing, the Ollune breed one, and their equivalent of an industry is a cultivated lineage of organisms doing the work at ambient temperature. Their drift-cities — **shoals**, in translation — are grown rather than built: living and once-living bladder architecture, tended, pruned, and extended across generations, buoyant by the same metabolic principle as their inhabitants and requiring the same unbroken attention.

It follows, and the Ollune state it flatly rather than apologetically, that they could never have reached space. No sequence of Ollune history ends in a launch vehicle. They did not join the Federation by arriving; the Federation arrived, and the first Ollune record of contact is filed in their alarm vocabulary, because from below a descending survey vessel is simply an object falling, and falling objects were the only category available.

## The Far Word and the Near Word

Ollune communication runs on two channels with opposite properties, and the entire structure of Ollune law is the consequence of not confusing them.

**The far word** is pressure: low-frequency sound, which in a dense hydrogen atmosphere travels several times faster than in Earth's air and ducts along the stable layers for enormous distances. It is instantaneous by Ollune standards, omnidirectional, unblockable, and audible to everyone in the band whether or not they wished to hear it. It also vanishes. Nothing recorded it, nothing carries the speaker's identity, and no one can prove afterwards what was said or by whom.

**The near word** is chemical: secreted markers read at close range, slow to compose, slower to disperse, individually distinct to the body that produced them, and persistent enough to be examined days later by someone who was not present. It requires physical proximity and mutual consent to a shared drift — you cannot mark someone who is not willing to hold station beside you.

Because these two channels are what they are, Ollune culture gave them opposite jobs, and no Ollune has ever needed the distinction explained. **The far word binds nothing.** Argument, news, poetry, insult, boast and alarm all live there, and none of it is testimony. **The near word binds everything.** Agreement, consent, debt, office and judgement exist only as near words, because only a near word can be attributed to a body and produced again later.

The consequences run deep, and two are worth stating for readers who assume the human arrangement is the only one:

An Ollune cannot be held to something they said. There is no such thing as a verbal contract, an oath, or a spoken confession, and the concept of being bound by one's word — in the far channel, the only channel that carries at range — reads to Ollune jurists not as honourable but as a category error other species have somehow built institutions on.

And an Ollune can always raise an alarm at no cost. Warning and committing are not two uses of one faculty; they are two organs. To cry out is not to accuse, not to volunteer, not to take responsibility, and not to be answerable for having been wrong. There is no Ollune equivalent of staying quiet because speaking up would mean owning the problem, and the species has never generated a word for it.

The mirror image is that a **false near word** is the gravest offence the Ollune recognise. Lying in the far channel is unremarkable and broadly expected; it is speech, and speech is not evidence. Falsifying a mark attacks the only channel anything rests on. Ollune serving off-world are noted as unusually and sometimes alarmingly relaxed about what other species say to them, and unusually literal about what other species sign — an asymmetry that has cost individual Ollune a good deal in dealings with human commercial counterparties, who tend to mean the opposite by both.

## Bands, Passings, and a Politics Without Territory

Cirrane's atmosphere is layered into zonal bands moving at different speeds, and a shoal belongs to a band the way another people's city belongs to a river. Two shoals in the same band drift at identical velocity and never meet, however close they look. Two shoals in adjacent bands are carried past one another on a fixed cycle — briefly, predictably, and years apart — because the bands beneath and above are moving at hundreds of metres per second relative to each other, and crossing that shear is the most dangerous thing an Ollune routinely does.

So Ollune politics is not territorial and never was. Nothing can be occupied, held, fortified, or taken. What exists instead is the **passing**: the window, sometimes only hours long, in which two shoals are within near-word range of each other. Everything that must be settled between them is settled in the passing or waits for the next one, which may fall outside the working life of everyone involved.

A civilisation cannot run on that unless its agreements are built to survive long silences without drifting, and Ollune institutions are shaped around exactly this problem — agreements written to be executed by parties who cannot consult, cannot confirm receipt, and will not learn for years whether the other side did what was marked. The Federation noticed the relevance immediately. A confederation of distant members in a cosmos with no faster-than-light communication has the identical structural problem, and had been solving it, by its own assessment, less well than a people who had never left their own sky.

## Federation Membership

Cirrane's membership is the Federation's founding sentence tested at full strength. Every other member world could have sent a delegation whatever the compact said; the Ollune could not, and the Federation's charter — that any sentient being's claim to material survival and self-governance is valid on its own terms, without requiring that being to first prove itself — was written to make that irrelevant. In the Ollune case it is not a generous reading of the charter. It is the only thing holding the seat.

The practical arrangement is imperfect and openly acknowledged as such. A handful of Ollune travel, in Federation-built pressure hulls maintaining a warm hydrogen envelope at Cirrane's temperate-shell conditions, at an expense no other member's delegates incur. Most do not. The Ollune position on any assembly matter is therefore usually carried rather than spoken, arriving by courier, always lagged, and read into the record by someone who is not Ollune. That the Ollune are the one member world whose delegate is generally not in the room is a standing item of Federation self-criticism, raised most often by [Mnemari](/star-rangers/lore/mnemari/) witnesses, who are constitutionally incapable of letting the assembly forget how long it has been raised.

The Ollune contribution to the [Ranger material-support compact](/star-rangers/lore/federation-of-sentient-beings/) is correspondingly unusual: not material, not survey data, but method. Ollune agreement-drafting practice — the discipline of writing terms that hold across parties who cannot confer — was adopted into Federation compact procedure generations ago and has since made its way, by a route nobody bothered to document at the time, into the [Star Rangers Safety Corps](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-safety-corps/) standards process.

The Corps has been rather less quiet about the other borrowing. The separation of a reported concern from a stated finding — the rule that raising a hazard is free, attributable to no one, and carries no liability, while a finding is signed, permanent, and belongs to the person who made it — is a Safety Corps procedural principle of long standing, argued for in Corps documents on its merits and defended in inquiry after inquiry. That an entire species had it as anatomy was noted, when the Corps found out, with what the record describes as professional irritation.

## Interspecies Notes

Ollune cannot tolerate standard humanoid life-support at any pressure, temperature, or composition, and no shared facility exists that both an Ollune and a human can occupy unsuited. Contact is therefore conducted through hulls and viewports, and the physical distance is permanent. The Ollune find this less remarkable than their visitors do; a species whose neighbours are reachable for six hours every eleven years has a settled relationship with not being able to get to people.

The Ollune and the [Chthonari](/star-rangers/lore/chthonari/) are frequently paired in Federation xenobiology as opposite solutions to low gravity, and get on, by most accounts, with mutual bafflement rather than friction. A Chthonari needs a sealed pressurised burrow and reads the world through the solid structure underfoot. An Ollune has never touched a solid structure and would not survive a burrow's pressure, and describes the Chthonari arrangement — enclosed, unmoving, in permanent contact with something that does not drift — in terms Chthonari translators have declined, politely, to render precisely.

Off-world Ollune report one experience with no equivalent at home, and report it consistently enough that Federation postings now brief for it. In a hull under thrust or in freefall, buoyancy stops mattering. Lift costs nothing. For the first time in their lives they can sleep without being held, and no one has to carry them. Some describe it as the deepest rest a body can know. Some cannot afterwards settle back into a hold, having learned that they can be set down, and the Ollune word for those who come home changed is old, gentle, and predates spaceflight entirely — it was first used of survivors recovered from a fall.
