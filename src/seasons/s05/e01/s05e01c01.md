:::pov qiren-tal

On Veyr-3 there is a proverb that does not translate well, because the load-bearing word is a verb humans do not have — the thing basalt does when it has cooled wrong and not yet said so. The nearest rendering: *the lattice knows before the surface agrees.*

The corridor had been saying so for six weeks.

Qiren Tal had walked the outer-ring junction after the maintenance consolidation, as the recertification requires, and struck the frame at the seventeen points their apprenticeship had made as automatic as breathing, and sixteen of the answers were the answers the corridor had always given. The seventeenth came back wrong. Not loud. Not urgent. Wrong the way a cooling flow is wrong a season before it shears — a resonance arriving a shade early on one axis and a shade late on the other, which in a symmetric frame is not two small errors but one large statement: the load is no longer being carried evenly, and something is quietly eating the difference.

The monitors disagreed. The monitors sampled the corridor at rest and between transits, found every figure inside tolerance, and produced the green column of numbers that the station's schedule was leaning on. Qiren trusted the resonance over the readout until both could be reconciled. This is not stubbornness. It is the correct order of evidence, and it has a price, and Qiren had been paying it daily for six weeks: a docked corridor, a slipping schedule with their name counted against every slipped day, and the particular loneliness of being the one instrument on the station that could not print its output.

The station's interim oversight had been, throughout, a man of great and genuine confidence, and he had spent it generously. He was confident in the monitors, and re-ran the certification suite twice; it passed twice. He was confident in the procedure, and offered to route the signature through a second specialist; the transit crews heard about it and declined the corridor, politely, in numbers. He was confident, finally and most warmly, in Qiren — confident they were overdue for leave, confident the assessment period had been stressful, confident that a recalibration of Qiren's own baselines might *put the whole matter to rest*. It was kindly meant. It was the only cruelty of the six weeks. Every form of his confidence was an answer to the question *is the corridor sound*, and Qiren's difficulty had never been that question. It was that what a Veyr knows arrives in the lattice, and the lattice holds no pen.

Then the new Line Captain came out to the ring, on her second day in the posting, and did the thing nobody in six weeks had done.

She did not ask why Qiren would not sign.

She asked what the corridor sounded like — and then she asked to be taught.

:::pov tissadelle

The file had reached her desk described as a personnel problem, which was the first thing about it she declined to sign.

She had read it on the transit out. Six weeks of green numbers. Six weeks of a specialist refusing them, at a daily cost anyone could compute from the duty roster — standing spent, schedule slip accruing against one name, meals logged alone at the end of shifts that kept getting longer. Her predecessor-of-sorts had tried command's whole confident repertoire: re-test, route around, reassure, and at the end, framed as kindness, the suggestion that the instrument doubting the station should be recalibrated until it stopped. Every one of those moves would have worked on a performance problem. None of them had worked. That was the finding, and it seemed to her the file's only real one: a problem that does not yield to confidence is trying to tell you it is a different kind of problem.

She knew the shape of it from inside. Three years ago, two decks up in this same station's review chamber, she had sat on the far side of a table from three careful people and been believed, narrowly, about a signal nobody's classifiers could see — and she had never once forgotten that the belief had cost twenty-two minutes of independent analysis and the whole distinction between what she knew and what she could show. Qiren Tal had been living on the wrong side of that distinction for six weeks, in a body whose knowledge arrives as resonance, on a station whose forms accept only figures.

She was not short of certainty herself. She had simply learned early what it was for. Certainty that needs a room agreeing with it spends other people; certainty that can stand alone can afford to listen. So she went out to the ring, and stood at the junction with her slate dark, and asked the specialist to teach her the seventeenth strike.

It took three transits. Qiren's forelimb rang the frame and named what came back — *early on this axis, late on that one, and the difference is the statement* — and on the third pass she caught it, not as engineering but as what it also was: a beat. Two resonances a fraction apart, interfering, the small dissonance living in the space between the notes. She had been trained to hear exactly that, years before the Rangers, in loading bays two systems away, and no part of that training appeared anywhere in her certification file either.

What remained was the part that was her job rather than her ear. She had acoustic pickups mounted at Qiren's seventeen points and set to sample *through* transit strain rather than between transits — the monitors' whole silence, it turned out, lived in that scheduling choice — and by the second cycle the figures said what the lattice had said in the first week: asymmetric loading, growing, seated exactly at the seal course the maintenance consolidation had replaced. The substitution was certified-equivalent, and equivalent, as Qiren observed with the first satisfaction of the whole affair, is a word for agreeing at rest. Under strain, the new seals carried the load a shade differently, and the frame was quietly eating the difference, and the crack that would someday have said so aloud had not yet been required to.

Nobody had done anything wrong on purpose. A procurement consolidation had merged two part numbers; a certification suite had sampled when the corridor was calm; a confident officer had answered the wrong question well. She wrote it up exactly that way — dysfunction, end to end, with no one in it to blame — ordered the substitution reversed, and signed the interim restriction under her own name, so that the last stretch of schedule slip would be counted against a Line Captain who could afford it rather than a specialist who had been paying for six weeks.

Two lines of the report she wrote slowly, because they were the ones the next station would need. That the refusal had been the station's most sensitive instrument, and should be filed henceforth as a reading requiring disposition, not a performance matter. And that the corridor had been full of confidence for six weeks — most of it genuine, none of it wrong, and none of it able to hear anything: confidence answers *am I right*, and the question on the ring had been *what is this costing the one who knows*.

Qiren signed on the first cycle after the reversal, the resonance and the readout reconciled at last, in the correct order.
