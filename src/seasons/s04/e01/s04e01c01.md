::::: scene 1

:::pov larsen

The case arrived the way Larsen's cases always arrived: as a document nobody was worried about.

The Chief Commissioner's office reconciles the five habitats' berth registries once a quarter. It is an administrative exercise — ledgers laid beside ledgers, a clerk confirming that the Compact's records agree with themselves — and it investigates nothing, because reconciliation was never designed to find wrongdoing. It was designed to find arithmetic. Which is why, when a clerk in Calloway's office found maintenance drone CM-4471 berthed at Eden and at a second habitat in the same hour, the finding went into the reconciliation report as an *entry discrepancy, referred for correction*, and would have died there, corrected, if reconciliation reports did not also cross Larsen's desk.

He read it twice. Then he pulled the quarter's full comparison and found it three more times. Four incidents, one drone identity, two habitats, zero overlap in either habitat's own ledger — because each ledger, read alone, was perfect. Eden's showed CM-4471 berthed, powered down, drawing keep-alive current, exactly where it said. The second habitat's showed CM-4471 arriving, working, departing, exactly when it said. Five competent registries, five hard edges, and a fact that only existed in the space between two of them.

That space was his jurisdiction. It was, in fact, the whole of his jurisdiction — the Governor had built Five-O for the cases that lived between the habitats' edges, and had never once needed to explain what that meant, because the cases explained it quarterly.

He sent the notifications the same afternoon. Both Commissioners, in writing: the case, the drone identity, the berths in question, and — on the Eden letter — the standard annex stating that Five-O's field observer would be flying the dock ring, dates attached. Larsen had heard every argument against announcing an investigation to the people being investigated, most of them from investigators he respected, and had the same answer for all of it. The habitats' own bureaus were not suspects; they were owed the truth about what was moving through their registries. And an observer nobody knows about was exactly the kind of quiet he had resigned a commission to get away from. If the fraud was clever enough to go still because a raven was announced, it was clever enough to have gone still already.

Privately, he suspected it would not go still. Whoever had built this had built it out of paperwork, and people who trust paperwork to hide them share a blind spot: they stop imagining that anyone will go and look at the thing the paper describes.

So he sent someone to look at the thing.

:::pov alala

The notification had gone to the Commissioner's office on Secondday. By Fourthday it had reached the dock crews, in the way written things reach dock crews: as a rumour with a reference number. By the time Alala came up the ring the first time, banded and obvious, half of Eden's dock shift had already heard there would be a raven, and the other half found out by pointing.

That lasted, by her own count, ninety seconds. It always did. A raven overhead is an event; a raven still overhead is weather. By her third circuit she had stopped being information and become part of the ceiling, which was the whole of the technique, and not one she had invented. Nobody hides. Nobody needs to. People do the forgetting themselves, on schedule, and the notification stays filed downstairs meaning every word it says.

Berth 60-Low held the thing the paper described: a maintenance drone, CM-4471 by its registry plate, powered down, drawing keep-alive current. She held station above it through two full shifts and let her subsystem keep the tallies, because tallies were what it was for.

The tallies were wrong in a quiet, accumulating way. A working drone wears where it works — scorch at the manipulator housings, scuffing along the docking collar, the thousand small insults of a machine that earns its berth. This hull had none of them. It had dust. It had, more precisely, *undisturbed* dust, a fine even film of the kind a dock lays down on anything that holds still long enough, and no machine on a maintenance rotation holds still long enough. The keep-alive draw never varied. The drone never left. The drone, she was increasingly certain, never *could* leave.

A shell, then. A hull with a registry plate, parked in a ledger's blind spot, holding a berth the way a coat holds a seat.

She filed the observation upward with the two photographs that mattered — dust, and the absence of scorch — and added the one operational note the Commander would want: the recertification inspection for this berth block fell in nine days, and a shell that could not power up could not pass it. Whoever kept this seat would have to swap the real CM-4471 back in before then.

Which meant that for one transfer window, both of them would be in the same place, and so would she.

::::: scene 2

:::pov alala

The swap came at the far edge of third shift, in the hour the dock ring runs thinnest, and it was — she noted this for the record with something adjacent to professional approval — beautifully ordinary. A tug, a tow cradle, a work order with a plausible number on it. Nobody hurried. Hurrying is what gets watched. The shell of CM-4471 went out on the cradle like any hull going to refit, and forty minutes later a drone came in wearing the same registry plate, and this one was scorched at the manipulator housings and scuffed along the collar and looked, in every way the first one had not, like a machine that worked for a living.

She did not intervene. Intervening was not her office. Her office was to be the Compact's own eyes on its own public dock, announced in writing, and to see what the ledger could not: that the plate and the hull had a relationship the certification record knew nothing about. She held her station in the girders and let the subsystem timestamp the sequence — out, interval, in — and found, in the part of her that predated the subsystem and had opinions about shiny objects, that the registry plate itself was the loveliest thing on the dock. Everyone below her trusted it completely. It was doing all of the work.

The tug crew looked up exactly once, the way the briefing had told them a raven was worth. One of them raised a hand, not quite a wave. She was, after all, official. Her presence was filed. There was a reference number.

Ninety seconds later they had forgotten her, and she watched them finish laundering a machine's identity in full view of the authority they had been formally notified was overhead, and she thought — in the way her thinking went, half tally, half instinct — that the Commander was right about paperwork people. They had read the notification. They had filed it. And then they had trusted the filing to do their imagining for them, which is the one job filing cannot do.

She left the ring the way she had come, obviously.

:::

:::pov larsen

The close, when Larsen wrote it, split the way his closes usually split: each habitat got the half that was theirs, and the seam went to the Governor.

Eden's half was the shell — a hull occupying a certificated berth under a registry identity it had no claim to, which was fraud against Eden's own dock authority and squarely Superintendent Oyelaran's bureau's to prosecute. The second habitat's half was the working drone and its work orders: eleven unmanifested cargo runs in a quarter, flown under an identity whose berth record said *powered down at Eden* the entire time. A drone that is officially asleep two habitats away makes an excellent courier. Nobody audits the movements of a machine the ledger says is not moving.

The seam itself — the part neither bureau could have seen, because each ledger read alone was perfect — went into Five-O's report, and Larsen wrote it plainly, because the mechanism deserved plainness: *a Compact registry identity attaches to the certification record, not to the hull.* The plate is a pointer to paper. Every check the dock runs, short of the physical recertification, reads the paper. Park a shell under the plate and the paper stays satisfied for a full inspection cycle, and the real machine is free to be anywhere, being anything, in the gap between what is certified and what is bolted to it.

He attached both of Alala's photographs. Dust, and the absence of scorch. Four registries, two Commissioners, one Governor's tasking, and in the end the case had turned on the difference between a machine that works and a machine that only holds still — a difference no ledger records and a raven reads at a glance.

The recommendation section was one paragraph. Registry reform was the Compact's business, not his; Five-O's office was the seam, not the statute. But he permitted himself the last line, because eleven years at a boundary teaches you which sentences are worth filing where a Governor will read them: *every part of this fraud was visible the whole time — from a vantage point that belonged to nobody. We should assume it was not the only thing parked there.*
