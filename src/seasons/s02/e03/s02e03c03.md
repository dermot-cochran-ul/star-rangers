::: pov sorcha-nic-dhiarmada

The delegation lifted the morning after the crossing, which Sorcha thought was the right way round to do it: let a guest's last night on the world be the world's best one, and load the shuttle by daylight with the sky's accounts settled.

Loading ran to the list, as before. The instrument cases went up the chain of hands, and the crates, and the delegation's mail, and somewhere in the middle of all that helpfulness — inevitably, the way water finds the one low place in a wall — went the other thing. Nobody carried it. It was *helped*, hand to hand, up toward the hold, by people who would each have said they were only passing along what somebody earlier had cleared.

She had wondered, these past weeks, whether she owed the guests a word about it, and had decided the custom answered the question both ways. You did not embarrass a guest by noticing them too accurately — but the thing at her gate had been told, in front of witnesses, that it was not from this house, and a thing that is not from the house is not a guest. So at the foot of the ramp she gave the quartermaster the plain accounting, in the tone she used for weather, which was the only tone she owned.

"You're carrying two."

He looked at her. He looked at the chain of hands, and at the small warm shape moving up it, and she watched the sentence arrive somewhere real in him — he was the one who had been relieved in the yard, that first day, in a way he couldn't explain. He wrote it on his slate. He said he would note it on the manifest. She believed him, and she also believed — with a shepherd's exact confidence in how a gate left open behaves — that the note would travel exactly as far as notes travel, and that the hands at the far end of the journey would be as helpful as the hands at this one. You cannot fence a valley with a memo. She had told the truth at the ramp because the truth was hers to tell, and what a habitat did with it was the habitat's own trade.

The delegation's rabbit boarded last, properly, in her carry-crate, on the manifest line that had been hers all along. At the top of the ramp she turned once and looked back — not at the valley, Sorcha noted, and not at the sky. At her. One colleague's look, handed over the way her collies handed a flock back at the gate: *your watch again.*

Sorcha raised a hand to it, spare as the custom allowed. Then the shuttle went up, and the valley got on with the morning. Bran trotted out to the lower gate and checked it, found nothing standing where nothing should stand, and checked it again every day for a week anyway, which was either a working dog closing a file or a working dog missing a colleague, and Sorcha, who could read every animal on this world, declined to decide which.

::: pov bubochka

The last page of an exercise log, Barsik taught, should answer the question you were sent with. Bubochka had been sent with: *observe how a world watches its own sky.*

She wrote her answer on the shuttle, while the valley fell away below.

The world watched its sky by turning its own lights out. That was the whole method. Not better instruments, not more attention — *less noise*. The valley put out everything of its own that shone, once a crossing, all together, so that the only thing performing was the sky. And a place that practises putting its own light out, kindly, on a schedule, is a place where no small warm thing can ever get a foothold — because a foothold needs somebody who has forgotten how to go dark. The two findings were one finding. She suspected Barsik had known that when he chose the exercise, and had not told her, because if he had told her she would have observed that, and nothing else.

The boy from the kitchen-calendar house had come to see her off. He gave her a copy of the crossing calendar, folded small, the red hours marked in his own hand, in case Eden wanted one. She had accepted it with proper ceremony and packed it herself. Eden did not have a sky to watch. She was beginning to think that might be the trouble with Eden, filed under a heading she didn't have yet.

Muffin came aboard mid-load, the way it had come everywhere: passed along. She watched from her crate and logged the time. She heard the warden say *you're carrying two* at the ramp, and logged that as well, and then logged what the sentence changed about the loading, which was nothing. That was the finding that mattered, and she wrote it carefully, because it was going in the report's last line and last lines had to hold weight without wobbling:

*The counter works person by person. It has never yet worked institution by institution. The warden told the truth to the whole chain at once, and every hand in the chain stayed kind.*

Filed. Not volunteered. Home by end of shift.

The trainee's reports arrived through the network in the usual way — logged, not volunteered — and Barsik read the whole file on the gantry above the receiving dock while the shuttle below finished pressurising, because a report about a loading chain is best read directly over one.

The unloading went as her last line predicted. Crates, cases, mail, and the specimen, down the chain of helpful hands, ashore on a habitat that had missed it without knowing anything was gone. The quartermaster's manifest note was real; Barsik confirmed it later from the cabinet top. *Possible second animal aboard, per Drithane warden.* It had been read at this end by a receiving clerk who had glanced down the chain, seen nothing that wasn't small and warm and already explained, and stamped the manifest through. The note had travelled exactly as far as notes travel. Barsik filed the whole transaction under the heading the trainee's report had built for it, and noted that the heading would bear more weight in time.

The specimen itself went to the laundry.

Not the galley door. The laundry — the place it goes to do nothing — for one full day, before resuming the Deck 7 approach as though no journey had occurred. Barsik held the observation a long time before writing anything against it. Class III does not rest. Class III does not need a day. He had no account of an animal that comes home from the first place it ever failed and takes a day to itself before going back to work — and, he noted, he had several accounts of colleagues who do exactly that, including the one writing this file. He recorded the behaviour, recorded that it read equally well as an animal decompressing and as nothing of the kind, and left the classification question precisely as disputed as he had found it, which was by now a professional discipline in itself.

Two additions to the working file, then, from one trainee's field trip.

The first: a new list, one entry long. *Environments the specimen cannot work.* Drithane, Gleann na gCaorach valley chain — where refusal is a kept custom and not a personality trait, where the resident cognition asks *where should you be* rather than *wouldn't it be easier*, and where the counter was performed, correctly, first try, by a warden who has never read this file and never needs to. A list with one entry is not a map. It is, however, no longer a blank page.

The second went into the trainee's log rather than the specimen's, and he considered the wording for some time, because she would read it. She had completed the exercise most agents never complete: she had observed what a report does after it is filed. Hers had moved a warden, kept a valley, reached a quartermaster's slate, and crossed a fold route home ahead of the thing it described. He wrote: *correct rate of progress*, and then, after consideration, a second line he had never before put in a trainee's log: *the agency's doctrine is two words. She has worked out the third, which is where the first two point.*

Below him the dock emptied. The specimen was back at the galley door by the evening shift, small and warm and given things, and somewhere on this habitat was a fifth person who could stand being rude for four seconds in public. Barsik had a list for that now, too. He was in no hurry. The specimen, whatever it was, was patient.

So was he.
