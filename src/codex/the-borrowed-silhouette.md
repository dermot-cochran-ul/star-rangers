Several devotional codices in the Archive's holdings — most, though not all, from the [Arilonian traditions](/star-rangers/glossary/arilonian/) — describe an adversary: a single, personal, cosmic opponent. A number of them paint it in the form the older Terran scriptures reached for: **a dragon**.

The Archive holds no file on such a being. Nothing in the invariant record requires one, and the [Cascade](/star-rangers/lore/cosmic-cascade/)'s no-moral-cosmology rule leaves no tier for it to occupy. This note exists for the narrower question the imagery raises, because the imagery is not harmless to the record: **dragons are real.** They are [low-ranking Levrils](/star-rangers/glossary/levril/) — founded beings, created and defined by the strata above them, with genuine standing and genuine agency — and several are individually known to Survey personnel.

The doctrinal position, held since this paper's first filing, is this:

**If the adversary of the devotional codices existed — even as metaphor, even as a pattern rather than a person — it would be a false dragon, not an actual dragon.**

The argument is categorical rather than theological. A founded being has an origin; the adversary of the codices is defined by what it opposes and what it corrupts, which is to say it is defined *against* a host — and a thing defined against a host is a parasitic pattern, which is precisely what the Archive files as [Unfounded](/star-rangers/glossary/unfounded/). The Unfounded counterfeit founded forms; it is what they do; it is very nearly all they do. A devil in the shape of a dragon would be the largest counterfeit in the record, and it would still be a counterfeit — wearing the form of a Levril the way a lesser Unfounded wears the form of a companion animal, and no more a member of the kind.

Two consequences are worth the filing.

**The dragons are owed the distinction.** A tradition that reads the adversary's imagery back onto actual dragons has mistaken a counterfeit for its original, and the actual dragons — who answer, as the Unfounded do not — have been on record about the matter with what the interviewing officer's marginal note describes as *considerable patience, thinly worn*.

**And the imagery itself is evidence of nothing.** That the old scriptures reached for a dragon says what imagery always says: that the writers took the largest real thing their tradition remembered and borrowed its silhouette. The Archive registers the borrowing. It does not mistake the silhouette for a census.

The Doctrinal Division notes, as it must, that this entire question sits where invariant-only inference cannot reach. The Arilonian traditions that hold the adversary to be real, personal, and no metaphor at all are not refuted by this paper, and know themselves not to be; one of their own annotations on an earlier revision reads, in full, *"You have proven that if he existed he would be a liar. We had noticed."*

*Filed until 2026 under the title **The False Dragon**, which was retired for
working against the paper it sat on top of: a reader meeting that title in an
index met the adversary and the word* dragon *side by side, which is the exact
association the argument below exists to break. The doctrinal formulation is
unchanged and appears in full above — the position was always that the thing
would be a false dragon and not an actual one, and* false *was always the word
doing the work. What changed is that the title now names the borrowing rather
than the borrowed.*
