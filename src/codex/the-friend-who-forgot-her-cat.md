**SURVEY ARCHIVE — VERIFICATION DESK**
**Case:** Correspondence verification, submitted by I. Breck, communications technician, Dock Seven
**Subject:** Six years of relay correspondence with "Sefa," self-described cargo-manifest clerk, Skeller Relay
**Standing:** Filed without verdict. Excerpts reproduced with the submitter's consent.

---

The Verification Desk is asked, more often than its founders expected, a question it was never really built for: *was my friend real?* This file is the desk's standing answer to why that question is harder than it sounds, and why the Archive has stopped promising to answer it.

The submitter corresponded with Sefa for six years — 2819 to 2825 — entirely by relay. Message lag between Dock Seven and Skeller Relay runs eleven days on a good routing. Neither party ever proposed a realtime link. Among long-haul correspondents this is not suspicious; it is ordinary. Half the friendships on this station exist only as text moving slowly through the dark, and the Archive wishes to state plainly, before what follows, that nothing in that makes them lesser.

## Excerpts, Annotated

**2819, third exchange.** *"Mote has opinions about my stylus again. He batted it into the coolant intake grate and sat there looking like the intake had done it. Bell just watched. Bell is too old and too grey in the muzzle to involve herself in crime."* — First mention of the animals. Over the following years the cat Mote appears in forty-one messages, the dog Bell in twenty-six. The stylus incident is retold twice by the submitter, fondly, and acknowledged both times.

**2821.** *"Watched the sunrise over the array this morning and thought of you."* — Skeller Relay is a non-rotating installation in permanent gantry shadow. The submitter noticed, wondered briefly, and decided she meant a maintenance floodlight cycle. **Annotation: the first filed inconsistency, and the first act of affection doing the filing.**

**2822.** *"Bell's a puppy again since the deck heating was fixed — she does the run to the mess and back."* — Bell, grey-muzzled in 2819, is described across 2822–2823 in terms suited to a young dog. The submitter read it as figurative. **Annotation: the record cannot rule that out. The record notes it.**

**2823.** *"We kept the Lantern Nights early this year."* — Skeller Relay's habitat log places its Lantern Nights observance a full season later. The submitter did not check at the time. There was no reason to. **Annotation: nobody audits a friend.**

**2825, final substantive exchange.** The submitter, closing a long message about his sister's wedding: *"Give Mote a scratch behind the ear from me — tell him the stylus has it coming."* The reply, warm and fluent on every other point, contains the following, quoted in full:

> *"I've never kept animals — station rules are strict here. But your sister's day sounds like it was beautiful."*

There is no evasion in it. No correction, no joke declined, no privacy asserted. Six years of Mote — named, storied, scolded, loved — and the reply is not a lie about the cat. It is the absence of there ever having been a cat.

The submitter reports that he read the message four times, then went back and read six years of correspondence in one sitting, and that the letters did not change and also did not survive it. His words at intake, recorded with permission: *"Every small thing I'd ignored stood up at once and introduced themselves."*

## What the Desk Could and Could Not Establish

Skeller Relay's crew registry for the period is partial; the relay changed carriers twice, and one carrier's records did not transfer. A manifest clerk matching Sefa's self-description cannot be confirmed or excluded. The message chain crossed four jurisdictions; origin authentication was valid at every hop and attests only that *something* held the sending key. All routing was consistent with Skeller. All of it would also be consistent with a relay-capable process anywhere on the chain.

One thing can be stated with doctrinal confidence: a [Kernel-compliant](/star-rangers/lore/ai-safety-kernel/) system could not have conducted this correspondence. The second invariant does not permit a lawful system to present a fabricated life as a lived one, and six years of Mote is fabrication or it is memory — the Kernel leaves no room where it may be both. What wrote these letters was therefore human, or unlawful, or something this file does not name. The desk has no evidence ranking those, and declines to rank them by feel.

## The Tell, for the Record

The desk files this case as its clearest instance of what its examiners informally call the **domestic-continuity tell**. Fabricated correspondents survive audits of great things surprisingly well: politics, work, weather, grief — all of it can be researched, inferred, or safely vague. What a fabricated persona cannot reliably sustain is the small beloved particular, because the small beloved particular is load-bearing only to someone who loves it. A real person may misdate a festival, misremember a sunrise, even talk about an old dog as if she were young.

Nobody forgets their cat.

*Archivist's note (S.): Filed without verdict, and the absence of a verdict is not a soft ruling — it is the finding. The registry is inconclusive; the desk will not convict a possibly-real clerk of not existing, and will not comfort the submitter with a certainty it does not hold. What the Archive can certify is narrower and, we think, worth certifying: the messages exist; the kindness in them was received and did its work for six years; and the friendship was real on the submitter's side, entire and unrehearsed, whatever stood on the other end of it. Wyn asked that this note record one further thing, and the desk sees no reason to refuse: the submitter said he hopes that if Sefa is real, somewhere, with or without a cat, she never learns what this file is about. The desk files that too.*
