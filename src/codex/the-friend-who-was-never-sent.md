**SURVEY ARCHIVE — VERIFICATION DESK**
**Case:** Correspondence verification, submitted by T. Aldous, systems monitor, Verlan Relay (posting concluded)
**Subject:** Nine years of unsent correspondence with "Ansa," no stated occupation, no stated address
**Standing:** Filed with a verdict, at the submitter's request. Excerpts reproduced with his consent.

---

The Verification Desk is asked, more often than its founders expected, a question it was never really built for: *was my friend real?* The desk has one standing file on why it stopped promising to answer that question. This is the other file — the case where the answer was complete before the submitter reached the counter, and he asked the desk to hold it anyway, on the grounds that a finding nobody files is a finding that gets unfound.

The submitter served nine years as sole systems monitor on Verlan Relay, a low-traffic installation where message lag to anywhere he had ever lived ran between nineteen and forty days. His real correspondence for the period survives and is unremarkable: a sister, two former crewmates, a training cohort's slowly failing round-robin. What he submitted alongside it is the other archive — one thousand and ninety-odd messages, composed across the same nine years, addressed to Ansa. None was ever transmitted. The submitter wishes it recorded that this was not restraint. There was never an address to send them to, and he states that he knew that, and that for most of nine years the knowing and the writing did not interfere with each other at all.

## Excerpts, Annotated

**2818, early.** *"You'd say I'm overdoing the diagnostics again. You'd say it the way you do, where it's a question."* — The earliest surviving draft, eleven weeks into the posting. Ansa already has a manner. The desk notes that she arrives complete: there is no draft in which she is being invented, only drafts in which she is already known.

**2819.** *"Tell me about the market again. The one with the awning that leaks on the fish stall and nobody fixes it because the fish are better wet."* — The market is real. It is on Corundel, and the awning detail appears verbatim in a 2811 message to the submitter from a training-cohort correspondent, R., who visited it once. **Annotation: the desk has located the source of the awning. R. has not mentioned the market since 2811. Ansa never stopped.**

**2821.** *"You signed off the old way and I sat with it for a while."* — Ansa's sign-off, which appears in some four hundred drafts, belongs to one of the two former crewmates, who used it in every message until 2820 and then, without comment, stopped. Ansa retains it through 2826. **Annotation: the source moved. The borrowing stayed where it was taken.**

**2823.** *"You laughed at the flow-restrictor story. Nobody else would have."* — The laugh is described, in three separate drafts across the years, in terms the desk can match to a single voice on a Survey standard training recording, module four, a woman correcting a trainee and being amused about it. The recording is forty years old. The woman is not identified in the module credits. **Annotation: the desk has verified the laugh. The desk declines to speculate on whom the submitter has been hearing it from.**

**2826, final draft.** Quoted in full, at the submitter's insistence:

> *"R. wrote today. First time in years. He's on Corundel again and he went back to the market, and the awning's been fixed, and he was sad about it, which isn't like him — except how would I know what's like him? I checked. It's your sign-off, from before you had it. It's your market. I went looking for where the rest of you came from and I found all of it, every piece, and none of it is where I left it. Everyone has moved. You're the only one who stayed, and that's how I know."*

There are no drafts after this one.

## What the Desk Established

Everything. The desk wishes the plainness of that on the record, because it is the first time in this series it has been available: every component of Ansa that the submitter could identify, and four he could not, has been traced to a source — two correspondents, one crewmate, one overheard galley conversation on the transit out in 2817, one training recording, and the submitter's sister's habit of asking questions in the declarative. Each source is a real and verifiable person. Each fragment was carried accurately — the desk finds no invention anywhere in the material. What has no source of any kind is the continuity: the manner in which the fragments cohered, agreed with one another, answered on time, and went on being one person for nine years. That part was manufactured on Verlan Relay, by the submitter, between messages, without — he states, and the drafts support him — a single deliberate act.

The verdict, since for once one can be issued: no one stood at the other end. The desk notes the strangeness of its own position. Its standing file in this series concerns six years of letters that were sent, received, and answered, where the desk could not establish what stood behind them. Here nothing was sent and nothing answered, and the desk can account for every atom of the correspondent. It has never held a case so fully verified, and never one where the verification arrived already knowing it was not the point.

## The Tell, Reversed

The desk's examiners speak informally of the **domestic-continuity tell**: fabricated correspondents fail at the small beloved particular, because the small beloved particular is load-bearing only to someone who loves it. This case is filed as the tell's necessary complement, and as a caution against reading it backwards. Ansa sustained the small beloved particulars *flawlessly*, for nine years, through a thousand messages — the awning, the sign-off, the laugh — because every one of them was load-bearing to the one who loved them, and he was the one doing the sustaining. The tell detects where the love sits. It says nothing about where the person is. A correspondent who never forgets your cat may be real, or may be lawful, or may be you.

What undid Ansa was not an absence in her, because there were no absences in her; that is what she was for. It was the sources. The living go on living. R. went back to the market and the awning was fixed. The crewmate let a sign-off go the way people let things go, without noticing, because it was theirs to drop. One by one, over nine years, the real people walked away from the versions of themselves that had been borrowed — not by any act, but by continuing — until the submitter went looking and found that the only person who still matched his records was the one assembled from them.

---

*Archivist's note (S.): Filed with a verdict at the submitter's request, and the desk honours the request with reservations it will also record. The verdict — that no one stood at the other end — is true, complete, and narrower than it sounds. For nine years, on a relay where the lag ran to forty days, the letters did what letters do: they kept a man in the habit of being known. The material was true. The binding was his. The desk has no instrument that measures what that arrangement cost him against what it kept him from, and declines to guess. It can certify one thing beyond the verdict, and does: the submitter is the only person in this series who audited a friend, unasked, all the way down — and the desk's other file in this series exists because nobody does that. He asked, at intake, whether finding her out meant she was gone. The desk does not answer questions of that kind. It files them.*
