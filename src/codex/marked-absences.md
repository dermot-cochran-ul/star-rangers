*Circulated for comment, not for citation. A living list: entries are appended as gaps surface and annotated as they close. Nothing here is ever deleted, including the closed ones — a record of what we once did not know is still a record.*

---

Every archive is judged on what it holds. It should also be judged on how honestly it handles what it does not, and this paper is my desk's answer to that second question.

The doctrine is short enough to state in one line: **mark the absence; do not explain it.** "The file is thin" commits this desk to nothing but the truth. "The file was lost in the evacuation" commits the record to an evacuation, a loss, and a cause — three claims, none verified, all filed under the appearance of humility. An unexplained gap invites a better-informed colleague to fill it. An explained gap invites the record to defend a guess forever. I have reconciled enough incident reports to know which of those costs more, and I have argued the underlying discipline at length elsewhere in this series — [Three Disciplines of the Record](/star-rangers/codex/three-disciplines-of-the-record/) — so this paper only applies it: we never overwrite, we append, and that goes for our ignorance too.

The second point of doctrine follows from the first: **a marked absence is itself information.** A reader who meets an unmarked gap cannot tell an oversight from a silence, and will reasonably assume the worse. A reader who meets a marked one knows three things at once — that the gap is real, that someone competent has looked, and that looking is not finished. That is not nothing. On some shelves it is the most honest thing we hold.

What follows is the working list, one entry per absence, each with the only two things a mark requires: where the record stops, and what kind of stopping it is. They are not all the same kind, and the difference is worth a reader's attention.

## The *Patience First* terminus — sealed

The record states, and states publicly: the vessel [departed in 2714 UCSD](/star-rangers/timeline/2714-patience-first-departs/), was found in 2732, and the crew were alive. What they encountered at the terminus is held in the classified record, and the public record says so in as many words. This is a **sealed** absence — the rarest kind on this list, and the least troubling to an archivist, whatever it does to everyone else. Nothing is missing. The account exists, its shelf-mark exists, and the authority that sealed it has never once pretended the seal is anything but a seal. I have argued for the reopening of smaller files and won every time. I have not argued for this one — not because the arguments fail, but because an honest closure can afford to wait, and on the evidence available to this desk, this one is honest. The mark stands so that no reader mistakes discretion for loss.

## The two Brother Daires — unsettled

A scribe named Brother Daire set down the [Life of Saint Aoife at Cill Aoife in 1216 AD](/star-rangers/timeline/1216-life-of-saint-aoife-set-down/) — [the document itself survives in this record](/star-rangers/codex/life-of-saint-aoife/) — and no birth or death date for him survives in any form. A keeper named [Brother Daire](/star-rangers/characters/brother-daire/) works an abbey on the Boyne fourteen centuries later. No record that has surfaced settles whether they are one man, a later namesake in the same tradition, or a title carried by whoever holds the role. This is an **unsettled** absence, which is the commonest kind and the least alarming: nobody is withholding an answer; the record simply never took one in. The correct filing is the one the character record already carries — state the question, refuse to answer it, and wait. Unsettled gaps close on their own schedule or not at all, and an archivist who hurries one is manufacturing a fact.

## The specimen the record cannot classify — live

The Undercover Pets working files include a specimen that the record, as of this pass, [still cannot classify](/star-rangers/threads/undercover-pets/) — this despite a taxonomy built specifically for [organisms that defeat the usual one](/star-rangers/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy/). This is a **live** absence: the file grows and the classification does not, which is a more interesting condition than either alone. I mark it rather than summarise it, because summarising an open investigation is how an archive quietly closes one. The gap is the finding, for now.

## The Hall of Quiet Instruments — provisional

An audio recording in this Archive's own stock — [the transcript is filed](/star-rangers/codex/hall-of-quiet-instruments-dialogue/) — recovered with speaker tags reconstructed from voiceprint, a location given only as "Hall of Quiet Instruments" that no station manifest has yet confirmed, and an attribution — to this desk — with a match confidence listed as *provisional*. It was filed anyway, on the doctrine that a provisional record is still a record. I include it here for the sake of the part I can add: the Archive's own rules do not permit the subject of a provisional attribution to be the one who confirms it, and I decline to be the first exception. So the mark is **provisional**, it is about me, and it stays exactly as filed. If that strikes the reader as an archivist protecting a mystery about their own desk, I can only answer that the alternative is an archivist certifying one, which is worse.

## The number of Concordants — unverifiable

The [glossary states the position](/star-rangers/glossary/concordant/) more carefully than I can improve on: the design account on file gives an answer, and the Archive still cannot, because no instrument can reach the question. This is the strangest genus on the list — an **unverifiable** absence, where the gap is not in the record but in the reachable universe. The honest filing is both halves together: the account, and the statement that nothing can audit it. I keep it on this list as a standing reminder that verification has an edge, and that pretending otherwise would be the least archival thing this desk could do.

---

Five entries, five different kinds of stopping. That is the point of the paper. A shelf with one kind of gap has a problem; a shelf with five kinds has a discipline. Readers finding an absence not listed here are invited to report it — the list is living, and the fastest way to shame this desk into an append is to find one it missed.

— *Sen, Senior Archivist. Filed to the working-paper series; comments, and absences, to the usual shelf.*
