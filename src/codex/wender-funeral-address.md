*A note on what this document is. It is a transcript of a eulogy, delivered at a service the speaker did not ask for and had spent eleven months declining to make possible. Wender never signed the confirmed-loss citation. The Oversight Council held the funeral anyway, as it was entitled to, and asked her to speak, as it was entitled to. She said yes within the hour. What she did with it is on the record below, and the Corps has been arguing about it ever since.*

---

**Delivered 2832 UCSD, Mid-Autumn, Threshold Station. Approximately eleven months after Dock Seven.**

---

I was asked to say what she was like, so I am going to do that, and then I am going to say one more thing and sit down.

She was the most careful officer I have ever commanded. I want to be precise about the word, because people hear *careful* and think *cautious*, and she was not cautious. She was careful the way a good instrument is careful. She would not tell you a thing she could not stand behind, and she would not decline to tell you a thing because it was going to be unwelcome, and in nineteen years of command those two habits have almost never turned up in the same officer.

She was not warm in a room. She was extraordinarily warm to work for, which is a different quality and a rarer one, and nobody who only met her at a briefing ever found that out.

She read everything twice. Once for what it said and once for what it was for. I have caught myself doing it since and I did not learn it from her; she learned it from somebody and taught it back to me without either of us noticing.

She kept a rule she had no reason for, because it came from her mother's hill and her mother had kept it, and she would not invent a reason to make it easier to explain. I thought that was a charming eccentricity for four seasons. I have come to think it was the whole of her method. Most of us cannot hold a thing we cannot justify. We resolve it, and the resolution is usually a lie we have agreed to stop noticing. She could hold it. She held a great many things at once and never once tidied them.

At Dock Seven she convened a Field Triumvirate by the clause, in the open, on a log built to be second-guessed, and she made each of the three conditions stand on its own before she took the next. She stated, on that log, that the thing she was acting to protect was not the animals, because they had no standing and she was not empowered to act for them and would not claim she was. She got eleven people onto four lifts in forty-one minutes.

The order she gave herself afterward was correct. I have checked it more times than is good for me, looking for the place where it stops being correct, and there is no such place. That is not a comfort and I am not offering it as one.

---

Now the other thing.

I have not signed the report that says she is dead. I am not going to sign it today either, and I would rather say so here than have it discovered later and treated as a scandal, because she would have loathed that and because you are all entitled to know what you are standing in.

I want to be exact, because she would want me to be exact, and because a great deal of nonsense has been said in eleven months by people who loved her and by people who wanted the file shut, and the two groups are not as separate as either would like.

I do not claim she is alive. I have no finding that supports it. Anyone telling you the Corps is secretly certain of anything is speaking beyond the evidence, and I will correct them by name.

What I have is this. The station recovered no body. *No body* is not a synonym for *gone*. It is a synonym for *we did not get everything back*, and that is a larger sentence and a harder one, and the smaller one closes faster, which is exactly why it is the one being offered to us today.

So I will not tell you she is dead, because I do not know it. And I will not tell you she is out there, because I do not know that either, and telling you would be the same failure wearing kinder clothes.

I am going to stand here and hold both, without resolving them, for as long as it takes.

She taught me how. I am aware of the irony and I am going to sit with that too.

---

If you want to honour her, do not do it by finishing her story. That is the one thing she never did to anybody and the one thing she would not forgive us for doing to her. Do it the way she did it: say what you know, say what you do not, keep the difference where anyone can see it, and let the record stay open as long as the truth requires.

That is not a smaller thing than a hero's citation. It is the only thing we have ever had that a citation is a substitute for.

Thank you. That is all.

---

*Archivist's note (S., Survey Archive): The Council's ceremonial file records this address as delivered and does not reproduce the second half. Three newsnets did, in full, within the week, and it is the second half that is quoted. I have filed the complete transcript here because a ceremonial record that ends at "forty-one minutes" is not a shorter version of this document. It is a different one.*

*I will note one thing that has not been remarked on anywhere I can find. She said "eleven people onto four lifts." The Guild's [loss adjustment](/star-rangers/codex/dock-seven-loss-adjustment/) says eleven crew in four lifts. She had read the claims file of a commercial courier concern before writing a eulogy. Nobody asked her to. She checked.*
