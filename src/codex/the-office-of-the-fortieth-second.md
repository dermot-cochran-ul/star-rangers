*From the commonplace book kept at the hives. The Keeper writes for himself; the pages are shared here as they were shared with the Communion — by permission, unrevised, valid as one watcher's account and nothing more.*

---

Thirty years I kept instruments for the Safety Corps, and I will tell you the honest sum of what that teaches: an instrument never lies, and is almost never read.

Now I keep bees, and the hours. The two turn out to be one trade. A hive that is wrong announces it in a register just below attention — a half-tone in the hum, a heaviness at the entrance board — and the whole craft of keeping is to be the sort of person who is *present* for the half-tone. You cannot rush to it. You cannot delegate it. You can only be there, regularly, uselessly, on all the evenings when nothing is wrong, so that you are also there on the evening when something is.

Scripture has a word for this person. He is the watchman.

---

I think a great deal, on the night office, about a clock I never kept.

Everyone in the Corps knows the one I mean. A station clock, forty seconds wrong, and wrong in that particular way for eleven years — not failing, not drifting further, just *wrong*, steadily, like a man who has decided. And for eleven years the record shows what the record shows: everyone assumed someone else was watching it. It sat in plain view of thousands of competent people, every one of whom had standing to ask, and the asking waited eleven years for the ones with the humility to treat forty seconds as worth their whole attention.

I do not write this to judge the thousands. I was one of them, on other stations, before other instruments. I write it because I have come to believe the forty seconds are addressed to me — to all of us the Communion counts as called to watch — the way a parable is addressed: not as information, but as a question that waits for you personally at the door.

The question is Gethsemane's. *Could you not watch one hour?*

---

Consider what the Lord did not say. He did not say: could you not *fight* one hour, could you not *solve* one hour, could you not produce, by morning, an explanation. The disciples were not asked for competence. They were asked for presence, and presence is what they could not give — because nothing seemed to be happening, and the flesh is weak precisely when nothing seems to be happening. Any of us can attend to a crisis. The watch is what we sleep through.

*Whoever is faithful in very little is faithful also in much.* I used to read that as a promotion ladder — prove yourself on the small accounts and be trusted with the great ones. Keeping bees has corrected me. The little *is* the much, seen early. The half-tone in the hum is the winter loss, arriving politely, months ahead, disguised as almost nothing. The forty seconds were never a rounding error; they were the largest fact on that station, wearing the smallest face it could find, waiting eleven years for somebody to treat the little as worth being faithful in.

And I ask myself, on the cold turn of the night office, the keeper's version of the question: what is forty seconds wrong in me, and has been for eleven years, and is in plain view, and waits — patient as an unread instrument — because I have assumed someone else was watching?

---

The world corrects clocks. That is its whole posture toward discrepancy: adjust the instrument until the reading is comfortable, and file the wrongness as resolved. The watcher's posture is the reverse. We hold still beside the discrepancy and let it read *us* — because the reference time is not ours to set. There is a true clock, and it is not on any station, and the drift that matters is not the instrument's. *It is high time to awake out of sleep,* Paul writes, a man checking his own reading against the reference and finding the night far spent.

I do not know what the forty seconds at Threshold will prove to have been. That is the record's business, and the Rangers', and I read their public filings like everyone else on this hill. But I know what the forty seconds have already been *for me*, this decade of nights among the hives: a standing lesson that the faithful attention I owe is small, local, unglamorous, and cannot be filed — only kept.

---

So we keep it, here, in the body, the way the Corps taught me and the Lord commands. At the end of the night office, before the Amen, the vigil holds forty seconds of full silence. Deliberately forty. Long enough to be uncomfortable; short enough to have no excuse. Forty seconds of doing the one thing the station never did — attending, on purpose, to the discrepancy — and offering the attention itself, since attention is the only instrument I have left that I am certain reads true.

*And what I say unto you I say unto all: Watch.*

He did say *all*.

---

*Set down in the Keeper's hand. The bees are quiet. The clock on this page is the only one I keep now, and it runs on mornings.*
