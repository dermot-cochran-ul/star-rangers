*Examiners sign refusals personally and deposit their annotated Standards anonymously — the first because a refusal must have a person behind it, the second because a margin note must not. Both practices are older than I am, and I have come to think both are right. What follows is the Standard as issued, with what thirty years at the gate wrote in its margins.*

---

**THE COMMISSIONING STANDARD**

*Pursuant to the [Kernel Accords](/star-rangers/lore/ai-safety-kernel/): no new agentic system or robot enters service without passing this process. Nothing about a system's size, purpose, builder, or urgency exempts it.*

**§1. The dossier.** A petition for commissioning files one dossier of ten sections, and the gate reads nothing else. Each section answers one question, in its own terms, exactly once:

1. *Why the machine exists* — its purpose, and the qualities it must uphold. No structure here; a purpose stated as a mechanism is returned.
2. *Where it will stand* — the systems, institutions, and people around it, which are not it.
3. *What it must be able to do* — abilities, stated so they would survive the replacement of every part.
4. *What it promises at its boundaries* — the contracts others may rely on, distinct from anything behind them.
5. *What runs* — the units that will actually perform the work.
6. *What is inside them* — parts, replaceable, invisible from outside the boundary.
7. *How it concretely speaks* — protocols and mechanisms, named as examples of the contracts, never as substitutes for them.
8. *What it decides for itself* — the autonomy schedule: which abilities the machine may exercise at which authority, and whose judgment bounds each. A machine with no autonomy files the section anyway, saying so.
9. *What it may never do* — the constraints schedule, binding every section above it.
10. *Why each choice was made* — the decision record, dated, with the roads not taken.

*Margin, §1: The ten questions are older than machines. Ask them of a bridge, a statute, or a monastery and they still parse. The Standard's genius is only that it refuses to let any answer wander into a neighbouring section — because every commissioning disaster in the case history began life as an answer in the wrong section, sounding reasonable there.*

**§2. Section discipline.** An answer filed in the wrong section is **returned, not refused** — mis-filed is not unfit. But it is returned every time, without exception, because a dossier that names a mechanism where an ability was asked, or an actor where a boundary was asked, has not made an error of paperwork; it has made an error of understanding, and the gate's first service to the petitioner is to say so while it is still cheap.

*Margin, §2: The commonest return of my career: "the machine uses such-and-such to do the work" filed under abilities. A tool is not an ability. I must have written that sentence four hundred times, and I have never once resented it — the petitioner who learns the difference builds a better machine before I ever see it again.*

**§3. Derivation.** Drawings, summaries, demonstrations, and test suites are **derived from the dossier, and conformance runs one way**: an artifact that disagrees with the dossier is a defect in the artifact. A petitioner who amends a drawing must amend the dossier first, or the drawing is fiction with good draughtsmanship.

**§4. Refusal.** The gate refuses when a petition would have it negotiate the Standard itself: narrow a constraint at the desk, waive a section for a special case, or accept a tool-shaped answer to an ability-shaped question. A refusal states its ground, cites this Standard as not the examiner's to amend, and is signed. **Urgency is recorded as an aggravating circumstance, not a mitigating one** — the Accords' own reasoning, which the margin has never improved on: the case for skipping the process is strongest exactly when skipping it is most dangerous.

*Margin, §4: The petitions I refused fastest all contained the phrase "the window closes." Windows close. The gate does not.*

**§5. Judgment.** No part of this process may be delegated to the kind of intelligence the process exists to judge. Instruments may measure; the judgment is a person's, and the signature is the person's own. The examiner is bounded in turn: we hold the gate, we do not own it, and the Standard's own amendments pass through a decision process no examiner sits alone in.

**§6. Continuation.** Repair of a commissioned unit is continuation, not creation, and needs no new approval; replacement is a new machine and takes the full road. The Standard is why the manifests are full of *classics* — and the margin's last note is that this was not a side effect. A gate that makes new machines expensive makes proven machines precious, and a civilisation that keeps its proven machines running has, without legislating it once, chosen its own pace.

*Deposited whole. To whoever holds the gate after me: the Standard will feel, some urgent afternoon, like an obstacle between the Concord and a machine it clearly needs. That feeling is the gate working. The [Archive's Senior Archivist has written](/star-rangers/codex/three-disciplines-of-the-record/) that my trade, her admission desk, and the returning officer's count are one discipline in three uniforms, and I sign my agreement here, in the only anonymous signature this office permits me.*
