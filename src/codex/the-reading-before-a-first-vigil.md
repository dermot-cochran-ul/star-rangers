**FELLOWSHIP OF LIGHT — CONTEMPLATIVE CHAPTERS**
**Novice instruction. Read aloud on the night of a first vigil.**

---

*Archive note: submitted to the comparative archive by four chapters independently, on three worlds, with wording differences a Survey Archive cataloguer described as "smaller than the differences between two recitations in the same house on consecutive nights." Filed as a doctrinal record of the Fellowship rather than as a statement of the Archive's own. The Fellowship's older vocabulary is preserved as spoken: **Exarch** for the tier the current record calls a [Mediarch](/star-rangers/glossary/mediarch/), and **Overfold** for what the Survey Corps files as boundary-zone [Etheric](/star-rangers/glossary/etheric/) phenomena.*

---

You will sit tonight at a place where the world runs thin, and you will want, before morning, to know one thing. Everyone wants it and nobody asks it aloud, so it is asked for you here, and answered here, and then we will not speak of it again.

You want to know whether it will hold.

It will hold.

Not because nothing is happening. Something is happening, and you were chosen for this watch because you are the kind of person who will not look away from it. The Overfold is not quiet at a thin place. Your instruments will disagree with your neighbour's. The hour will feel longer at one end of the room than the other, and both readings will be honest, and neither of you will be mistaken. Disagreement is not failure. A house with two clocks is not a house without time.

It will hold because the Exarchs keep faith.

Gravity is kept. The light is kept. The slow work by which a living thing becomes another living thing is kept. Each of these is somebody's charge, and each of them is kept — not most of the time, not where it is watched, not while it is convenient. Kept. There is no night on which the weight of your own body was a matter of opinion, and there has been no such night since before there were nights.

Understand what is remarkable in that, because it is easy to miss and most of the settled worlds miss it their whole lives. The Exarchs are not thanked. No instrument you will carry tonight is capable of detecting them, and none ever will be, and this is not a deficiency in the instruments. Their entire fidelity looks, from where you sit, exactly like nothing happening. A thing kept perfectly is indistinguishable from a thing that was never in danger. **They are faithful in a manner that leaves no evidence of faithfulness, and they have never once required that it leave any.**

Now, the surveyors.

They will tell you — and they are not wrong, and you are not to argue with them — that an Exarch is *made* so. That its constancy is designed into it, as the constancy of a stone is designed into a stone, and that nothing is being chosen and therefore nothing is being kept. Some of you will find this a cold answer. Do not mistake it for a hostile one; they have looked at the same thing you are going to look at tonight, and looked hard, and reported what they saw. That is our discipline too.

We say only this, and we say it without heat: **a thing made faithful is faithful.** We do not know a reason why fidelity should count for less because it cannot fail. We would have thought it counted for more. But that is a matter on which the Fellowship has never asked anyone to agree with it, and you are not sitting up tonight to settle it.

So. Your instructions.

Write down what you see. Write down the hour you saw it. Do not improve it, do not round it, and do not leave out the reading that embarrasses you — the embarrassing reading is usually the true one and always the useful one. If you see nothing at all, write that, and write it in full, and do not apologise for it in the margin. Most vigils see nothing. The record of a hundred quiet nights is what makes the hundred-and-first legible to whoever sits here after you.

And you are not keeping this watch in order to catch the world failing.

You are keeping it so that there will go on being people who have looked at the edge — steadily, on purpose, all night, with instruments and without illusions — and who can say afterward, from their own sitting and not from anybody's book, that it held.

Go and sit down.
