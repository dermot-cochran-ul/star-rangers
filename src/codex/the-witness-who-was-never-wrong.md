**SURVEY ARCHIVE — VERIFICATION DESK**
**Case:** Statement-drift review; four statements by one witness concerning one evening
**Subject:** Bridie Withy, of Carrick Slip, concerning the evening of her neighbour's departure; statements taken 2819, 2822, 2826, 2828
**Standing:** Filed without a verdict. Opened at the desk's own instance, as a caution against its own instrument.

---

The desk's continuity work rests on an assumption so quiet that it took nine years and one witness to make us write it down: that an honest account holds still. We audit the small beloved particular against the person who loves it, and the audit means something only if the particular stays where the witness put it. This is the file on a witness for whom nothing stayed, who was sincere in every version, and who could not be shown the difference — because, as the desk eventually understood, from where she stands there is no difference to show.

Bridie Withy's neighbour walked out to the shore road one evening in 2819 and did not come back. The desk holds no view in this file on where he went; that matter is filed elsewhere and remains open. This file concerns only the evening, and only her account of it, which she has given four times.

## The Four Tellings

She gave her first statement within the month, and it reads as fresh statements do: ragged, hedged, in the right kind of disorder. He passed her wall just after supper, while there was light enough to see. She thought he carried the lantern from his porch, though she qualified it — *I think he had the lantern, he usually did*. He said goodnight over the wall, as always. Her dog barked after he had passed, which she mentioned only because the dog barked at everything.

Three years on, giving evidence to a boundary survey that touched the coast, she told it again. The lantern had become a case — *he carried a little case, I remember thinking he had somewhere to be*. The goodnight survived, but had grown shorter. The dog had moved closer to the centre of the account.

By the third statement the case was gone. He carried nothing; his hands were in his pockets; he said nothing at all, only lifted his hand as he passed. It was later than she had once said — *full dark, I only knew him by his walk*. And in the fourth statement, taken this year at her own request, the account has found its final shape: he never spoke, and that was what frightened her; the evening was dark; the dog — this is new — would not come out at all, and she has never seen him carry anything on the shore road in her life.

Set side by side, the four statements disagree about the light, the burden, the greeting, and the dog. What the desk wishes recorded is the direction of the disagreement. The account did not decay. It *improved* — each version more fluent than the last, better shaped, better lit for its purpose, the hesitations resolved, the ominous notes gathered where a story would want them. Her memory has been doing, slowly and in good faith, what any teller does to a tale: revising toward the version worth telling. Nothing in this is exotic. A memory is not a recording; it is a document revised at every reading, and she has been reading it for nine years.

## The Challenges

The desk put the earlier statements in front of her, in her own hand and over her own signature, and her replies belong in the file verbatim.

Of the lantern: *"Then it was taken down wrong."*

Of the goodnight, shown her signature beneath it: *"I may have signed that, but I never said it that way."*

Of the case, the light, the dog: *"What does it matter what he carried? He went."*

And at the last, when the examiner pressed the whole set at once: *"You weren't there."*

She is right. The desk was not there. But neither, by now, is she. The woman who was there made the first statement, and the witness has been revising her ever since. This is the finding, such as it is, and the desk asks that it be read without contempt: **she was not defending a lie against the truth. She was defending the only account she has against four strangers claiming to be her.** A witness is an archive that keeps only its current revision. The contradiction between versions is real, but it exists only where two copies exist — which is to say, only in this drawer. Asked to explain a discrepancy she cannot hold both sides of, she does what any archive does when handed a document it has no record of issuing: she rules it a forgery, or a clerk's error, or immaterial. Every dismissal in this file is the honest testimony of a person whose past versions genuinely do not exist for her.

The practice consequence has already been adopted, and this file is its justification: the continuity tell is to be read against *contemporary* copies only, taken early and in duplicate, and never against a retelling — however sincere, however sworn. Sincerity is not stability. The desk's instrument measures where the love sits; it must never again be pointed at where the memory has settled.

## The Residue

One detail did not drift. In four statements across nine years, in nearly identical words each time, Bridie Withy states that the shore road was dry that evening. The coastal record shows rain from mid-afternoon.

The desk knows what to do with a detail that wanders; the whole file above is what to do with it. It does not know what to do with a wrong detail that holds perfectly still while everything around it revises. That is not a memory behaving like a memory. It is a nail, and the desk does not know what it is holding down, and declines to guess in writing.

Filed open.
