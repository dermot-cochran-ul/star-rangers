# Scene draft — the expulsion order and the parting

**Status: draft, unpublished, story-bible only.** Not a chapter file, no front matter,
no `comment_id`. It is spoiler material for the Tír na nÓg expulsion (see
`tissadelle-arc-s6-7.md`), and it stays out of `src/` until the arc's timing is
settled and Dermot decides it should exist.

**Timing deliberately unfixed.** Nothing below names a season, a year, or a distance
from the 2831 croft visit. The only continuity it commits to is that this comes
*after* `s05e02c01` ("What the Hill Keeps"), which it leans on heavily.

**What it inherits from S05E02C01, on purpose:**

- The Union extends her "the correctness owed a visiting officer, nothing owed a
  daughter" — the expulsion is that sentence completed.
- Órla knows the shape of every sentence her daughter owns.
- Tissadelle corrects an inaccurate report before it leaves her desk, cleanly, aloud.
  The scene turns on the one time she does not.
- The Highland Guard is the Union's detail; its farewells are exactly what the
  charter requires and not one degree warmer.

:::pov orla

The order took ninety seconds to read aloud, and Órla had come down from the uplands for all ninety of them.

She had never been in the council house before. Six generations of her family had held the Boirinn croft without any of them needing to stand in a room like this one, and she found herself noticing the floor — a pale flagstone, laid in a pattern somebody had been paid to think about — because the alternative was noticing the clerk's voice, which was pleasant, and unhurried, and reading out the end of her daughter's right to come home.

The terms were not complicated. Standing withdrawn. Entry barred. Effective on departure, which was scheduled, because everything about this had been scheduled by people who had never met either of them.

Órla watched her daughter receive it the way she received a manifest.

That was the part nobody else in the room would have known to look for. Tissadelle stood with her hands loose and her attention level and her weight even on both feet, and to the clerk and the Guard and the two Union officers at the long table she must have looked like an officer accepting a lawful instruction, which is what she was. Órla had raised her. Órla had read four years of letters getting shorter and more exact and had learned the shape of every sentence the girl owned, and she could see that there was no sentence in this one at all. Her daughter had gone somewhere behind her own face and closed the door politely.

So Órla crossed the floor.

She was not a woman who did this. She knew it about herself and had never much minded — the croft did not reward display, and neither had her mother, and neither, in fairness, had she. She had spent forty local years keeping a rule nobody could explain and calling it a habit worth keeping, and in all that time she had not once made a scene in front of strangers.

Nobody stopped her. She understood, crossing, that nobody in the room had the standing to: the order was an administrative instrument, and it had authority over where her daughter could stand, and none whatever over who could stand next to her.

She took hold of Tissadelle and did not let go.

It was not quick. She had it in her mind, quite clearly and without any drama about it, that she was never going to leave Tír na nÓg — she had told her daughter so more than once, and neither of them had found it worth arguing over — and that her daughter was now not permitted to return to it. Both of those things were simply true, and together they made this the last of something. She had buried a mother and a husband and had held neither of them at the end, and she was not going to be quick about this one.

She felt the exact moment her daughter's arms came up.

:::

:::pov tissadelle

There was an error in the order.

It was small — a date given as the delivery rather than the arrival, two days out, of no consequence to anything the instrument did — and Tissadelle noticed it the way she noticed everything, immediately and without effort, in the third line.

She let it stand.

That was the whole of it, and she knew it while it was happening. A correction was a question, and a question was what she was standing here for. She had asked what the rule protected against, and then who had set it, and then who had held it since, and each of those had been a reasonable thing to want to know and the last had been a reasonable thing to want to know out loud, in a room, to a person who had not expected to be asked. She had been trying to help her mother. She had been, by the plainest reading of the record, doing her job with the training the Corps had given her, on ground where the Corps had no standing at all.

So she let a wrong date go into the permanent register of the Celtic Union of Planets, and did not open her mouth, and understood that this was the price and that it was cheap.

She was aware of the Guard at the door being correct. She was aware of her own posture and was keeping it, because the room contained four people who would file a report about this and one who would not, and the four were watching.

Then her mother was crossing the floor, and Tissadelle had perhaps two seconds to decide what to do with that, and discovered that she did not have to decide anything.

Her mother's hands did not read as weather. They did not read as anything. There was no shape in it to interpret, no habit to recognise, no sentence to hold up against four years of letters and check for drift — just a woman holding on, in front of strangers, for far longer than was seemly, in a room where seemliness was the only currency anyone had been trading in all morning.

Tissadelle brought her arms up and held her mother back.

She could not have written a report on it. She noted that, distantly, with the part of her that noted things: there was no observation here she could confirm, no measurement, nothing she could put her name to and file. It was the only thing in the room that was not procedural, and it was the only thing in the room that was true, and those had never once been the same before.

## Notes on the draft

**The correction is the load-bearing invention.** Her established habit is correcting
an inaccurate sentence aloud, cleanly, before it leaves her desk (S05E02C01). Here she
finds an error and lets it stand, because correcting it would be one more question —
so the thing that got her expelled is the thing she has to give up to survive the
room. Shown, never explained. Cut it and the scene is only sad.

**"Exactness is not the same as distance" is answered, not quoted.** Órla's line is
never restated. The reply is Tissadelle finding nothing in her mother's hands to
*read* — no shape, no habit, no sentence to check for drift — which is the one
transaction between them that exactness has no purchase on. If that lands, the S5
line pays off without either woman noticing it has.

**Órla moves; Tissadelle does not break.** Her composure holds to the last paragraph
and beyond. What changes is only that she raises her arms, and Órla feels the moment
it happens. Do not let a later draft give Tissadelle tears in front of the Guard.

**Open, per the arc file:** what the questions were actually about is gestured at —
the rule, who set it, who held it since — without committing to the Hollow. If the
Hollow is the answer, this scene needs no change. If it is not, three clauses adjust.
