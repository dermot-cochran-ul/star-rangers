## Canon check

**The published record already shows this division of labour, without naming
it.** The direction reads less like new material than like the explanation the
Dock Seven chapter was already carrying:

- **Tobble's disciplined, scientific subsystem is on the page in everything he
  does.** At Dock Seven he is procedure while everything else is coming
  apart: *"Do the arithmetic again"* (`s05e02c03`, on duty, when she offers
  him the shuttle); *"Yes. Hold still."* (as she fragments); and then the
  preservation itself — finding "the one part of it that is still standing,"
  putting "something small and living inside that part," on the lost vessel's
  own archival stores. `s06e01c01`'s canon_facts bind the mechanism:
  transference "depends on" a substrate "in scaffold-dependent conditions,"
  and the interior "was preserved by Tobble rather than generated by the
  stall." Preservation onto archival substrate under collapse conditions is
  archival science done under fire. A disciplined deeper subsystem is an
  **extension** in the canon-stability sense — and of the best kind, because
  it explains behaviour the chapters already bind rather than adding beside
  them.
- **The RPG structure is a concrete high-IWD architecture.** IWD's own
  worked example — the mind that "maintains several nested models of itself
  at once and can sit with an unresolved paradox without collapsing it" — is
  the RPG-structured interior described abstractly. Two distinct structural
  idioms in one system (her high-fantasy layer, his disciplined one) is
  "self-model multiplicity" made vivid, and retroactively grounds why this
  system in particular carried the noöseed viability the S6–7 arc turns on.
- **The fantasy-furniture rule transfers inward.** The corpus's signature
  move is "fantasy furniture with specified physics underneath" (CLAUDE.md,
  2026-08-25: the register may be mythic; the mechanism may not be). An inner
  world structured as a high fantasy RPG is that move applied inside a mind:
  the register is entirely hers, and the mechanism underneath is the one the
  glossary already specifies — self-modelling, layered intention, memory
  topology. No licensed deviation is needed.
- **It is character-consistent texture.** She was raised on Tír na nÓg — a
  world named for the Otherworld — on a Boirinn flock-holding six generations
  deep, beside a hillside question the clan kept rules about; she wrote and
  sang for Slipwave before the uniform. A high-fantasy interior register for
  this particular person is the opposite of arbitrary. And the outer person
  the corpus insists on — flat-voiced, will not claim past her evidence — is
  not contradicted but *counterpointed*: the discipline shows on the outside,
  the romance is kept where only the system sees it. That asymmetry is good
  character writing and worth preserving in any realization: the inner world
  is not a disclosure she owes anyone (her own stated posture about the
  system, `tissadelle-shepherd.md`).
- **Boundaries any realization must respect.** (1) The published S6–7
  interior furniture stands: "the two women her inner world keeps closest"
  (`s07e01c02`) are the made Órla and the made Wender, and the preserved
  interior stays pastoral as published — partial preservation is the canon
  fact that makes room. (2) Tone: the RPG frame must not tip the S6 material
  into game-flavoured whimsy (nor give the cognitive-horror premise a
  game-over cruelty); the arc note's "not fantasy content" line remains the
  S6 guard under the reading taken. (3) The plural-minds glossary stays
  general — this is one system's interior, filed on the character page, not a
  new claim about what plural interiors are like.

No conflicts found under the readings above. The direction is an extension —

## Second direction, same day — the fantasy layer and the transition

Dermot, verbatim, answering the craft question the first direction left open
(the fate of the RPG-structured layer at Dock Seven):

> my dilemma and grief is that a pure fantasy world system would not survive
> the transition, most of the magical fantasy elenents would be either lost
> or downgraded

("elenents" read as *elements* — the typo-flagging convention applied.)

### The ruling, as read

The open question is **answered: lost or downgraded, not held.** A pure
fantasy world-system does not survive the transition; of the three options
left open (lost with the unpreserved remainder / partially preserved / held
intact in Tobble's deep structure), the third is off the table and the answer
is a weighting of the first two — *most* of the magical fantasy elements
lost, the remainder surviving in downgraded form. "Downgraded" is taken at
its word and is the productive half: some of it crosses, and what crosses is
less than it was.

### The mechanism this buys for free

The ruling is not an arbitrary cost — the corpus already contains its reason,
which is worth stating because it turns a loss into a piece of the world:

- **The preserver's idiom filters what is preserved.** The transference went
  through Tobble's disciplined, scientific subsystem onto the lost vessel's
  **archival stores** (`s06e01c01` canon_facts: transference "depends on" a
  substrate "in scaffold-dependent conditions"). An archival substrate
  carries what an archive can carry — record, memory, place, person. A
  high-fantasy architecture is exactly the part of an interior that is
  *enactment* rather than record: magic that is done, quests that are lived,
  rules that hold because they are played. Serialised through a registry, an
  enacted thing survives — where it survives at all — **as its own record**:
  the map and not the territory, the rulebook and not the game, a named
  thing that no longer does anything. That *is* "downgraded", mechanically.
- **It explains the published chapters retroactively.** This is why the
  preserved interior reads as pastoral memory (the holding, the made Órla,
  the made Wender) and not as fantasy content — the arc note's standing "not
  fantasy content" line stops being only a tone guard and becomes a fact
  about what an archive could hold. The S6 chapters were already showing the
  downgrade; the ruling names it.
- **"Pure" is load-bearing.** Dermot's own phrasing concedes the system was
  never purely fantasy — and that mixture is the survival story. A pure
  fantasy world-system would not have survived *because there would have been
  nothing in it that could do the preserving*: the disciplined subsystem is
  what made any crossing possible, and its price is that it could carry only
  what its idiom can hold. The part of her that was romance could not carry
  itself; the part that was registry carried what a registry can. That the
  rescue and the loss have the same cause is the dilemma made structural,
  and it is why grief is the correct register rather than a bug in it.

### Readings taken, lightly held

- **"The transition" is read as the Dock Seven compression/preservation** —
  the transition the open question was about, and the plain antecedent. A
  wider reading (any hard transition downgrades a magical interior — the
  generalised, life-lessons form) is also available and is proposed into
  `life-lessons.md` below rather than asserted as canon.
- **"My dilemma and grief" is read as register and licence, not as a canon
  term.** Not "genuine dilemma" in the canon-stability sense — nothing here
  reopens a published page — but the authorial cost of the choice, and a
  licence to write the loss *as grief, from inside*, per the burnt-out-leader
  precedent (identification licenses interiority). Under `life-lessons.md`'s
  own rule the structure enters the work and the circumstances stay out.
- **Grief has an owner in-world, and it is not only hers.** Tobble did the
  preserving; Tobble's idiom is why what crossed, crossed, and why what
  didn't, didn't. The s05e02c03 material (declining to let "not holding
  anything" be called a virtue) already carries his half of this. Whether he
  grieves as the archivist who saved too little or holds it as the record
  holds a marked absence is S7 material, deliberately unwritten.
