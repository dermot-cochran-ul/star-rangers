## Third, same session — does church-space still need to be private?

Dermot, verbatim:

> does church-space still need to be private if it is clearly its own edition?

A question, not a direction, and answered in chat as an assessment. Recorded
here because it is undecided and because the answer is an engine change if he
wants one.

### What privacy does today that the ladder does not

`private: true` on the `church-space` thread (`lib/storyline-threads.js`)
hides it on **every build that does not name it in** — including the
unfiltered canonical site, GitHub Pages, local dev, and every third-party
fork, all of which resolve the default edition and carry no `THREADS` filter.
The tier ladder (`lib/editions.js`, 3 September) decides only what the
**branded** editions carry; it says nothing about an unfiltered build. So
today two mechanisms together produce "only the contemplative editions show
Church Space": the ladder puts the thread in `CONTEMPLATIVE_TIER`, and
privacy keeps it off the default build. Dropping privacy with nothing in its
place would put Season 8 on fianilchruinne.com, which the 3 September ruling
placed at the general tier on purpose.

Privacy also carries three pieces of special machinery: `homeDomain` (where
an excluded placeholder points, since the canonical site excludes the thread
too), `signatureTags` (the validator's guard against a church-space page
shipping untagged on a public domain), and the **public → private link
boundary** in `validate-content.js`, which refused the Reading Tiers page's
link to the thread's landing page the same morning — so the page names Church
Space in plain text.

### Three shapes, put as choices

1. **Keep it as it is.** Privacy and the ladder overlap but do not conflict;
   nothing is broken. Cost: two concepts where one would do, and the
   link-boundary rule keeps biting reader-facing pages that want to point at
   the contemplative tier.
2. **Replace privacy with tier gating.** Give the thread `tier:
   "contemplative"` and have `content-filter.js` hide any thread above the
   build's tier — the mechanism the `::: pov … tier=contemplative` blocks
   already use. Same visible outcome today (the two contemplative editions
   and the archive show it; the default edition is general, so the canonical
   site, GitHub Pages and every fork still do not). What retires: `private`
   as a concept, `homeDomain` (the placeholder can point at the tier's
   ranking host), the signature-tag check (membership by season and tag is
   unchanged, but the failure mode it guards is no longer a *privacy* leak),
   and the link boundary (a general-tier page linking into a
   contemplative-only page gets the ordinary excluded placeholder, like any
   other narrowed link). This is the coherent end state now that "belongs to
   one pair of domains" has become "belongs to the contemplative tier", which
   is exactly what 3 September made true. Cost: an engine PR with tests
   (`test/content-filter.test.js` pins the privacy truth table and would be
   rewritten, not deleted).
3. **Make it an ordinary thread.** Only right if the canonical site is meant
   to move to the contemplative tier and become the complete view. That
   reverses a ruling one day old, so it is listed for completeness rather
   than offered.

**Assessment given in chat:** the second is the shape the ladder implies; the
first costs nothing now. An engine change of this kind is second-tier work
under `CLAUDE.md` — propose, not do — so nothing was changed on the first
pass.

**RULED the same day — "replace privacy with tier gating."** Realized in the

## Fourth, same session — at least one alien thread or strand

Dermot, verbatim:

> At least one thread or strand featuring a set of alien protagonists

### What this says, in one line

The record is to gain at least one storyline — at the thread level or the
strand level, his choice left open — whose viewpoint is carried by an
ensemble of non-humans, not by humans with an alien beside them.

### What the corpus does today, measured

- **Five character pages carry the `alien` tag:** Mira of Brine, Qiren Tal,
  Sethka Ru, Suvra Kel and Thrum. The 3 September intake also counted Alala
  (young-adult site) and Thrum (children's) as aliens already appearing where
  chapters put them.
- **Alien viewpoint prose is a handful of POV blocks.** Suvra Kel's POV is
  still a story-bible draft (`scene-draft-one-warmth-in-the-room.md`). No
  published chapter is carried by a non-human ensemble; the nearest shape is
  Undercover Pets, whose cast is animal rather than alien.
- **Species with the most settled design today,** by the repo's own record:
  the Krenyi (the "only other humanoids" ruling, and the body-plan pattern
  `species-design.md` derives from), the Prismeri (body derived from Prismere,
  3 September, images regenerated to it), and the Pandoids (four rulings and
  Fliade drafted, 16 August). Everything else undescribed is bounded by
  `species-design.md` rather than written.
- **Seasons 0–9 are claimed.** A new thread takes Season 10 under the
  standing "create additional seasons as needed" direction (5 August). A
  strand takes no season of its own: it lives inside one and meets the other
  strand at a convergence fixed before either is drafted
  (`story-bible-summary.md`, "What makes two strands independent").
- **The alien edition is open** (3 September, fifth direction; three shapes
  offered, none chosen). Its third shape was *wait until there is enough
  alien-viewpoint prose to fill a site.*

### Readings taken, flagged for confirmation

- **"Thread or strand" leaves the level open on purpose, and the two cost
  different things.** A thread is an engine registration — an id in
  `lib/storyline-threads.js`, at least one season, a tier placement, a
  `/threads/` landing page — and, if an edition follows, a domain and a face.
  A strand is story-bible-only: no id, no page, no registry; it lives inside
  a season of an existing thread beside a human strand, and its convergence
  point is decided first. **Confirm that either satisfies the direction, and
  that the level is his to pick per the options below.**
- **"A set of alien protagonists" is an ensemble carrying the viewpoint, not
  one alien POV among humans and not aliens as support.** Whether the set is
  one species or several is not said. **Confirm; and say whether one species
  or mixed.**
- **Tier placement defaults to general.** The 3 September fifth direction
  puts the aliens with the general reader, so a new alien thread would be
  added to `GENERAL_TIER` and inherited by the contemplative tier by
  construction. Nothing forbids an alien strand in a young-adult season if
  the protagonists are young in that tier's years — the life-stage rule
  applies to aliens as to anyone. **Confirm general as the default.**
- **This turns the alien edition's "wait" into a programme.** The edition
  still waits on a domain and a face, and that decision is unchanged; what
  changes is that the prose it would need is now ordered rather than hoped
  for. The edition can follow the thread when the thread can fill a site.
  **Confirm that the edition decision stays open and separate.**
- **The trope default applies in full.** An alien ensemble is where the
  well-known shapes crowd in — hive minds, one-hat species, the noble
  outsider, the human-with-prosthetics — and the standing rule refuses them
  by default in prose and in image prompts alike. `species-design.md` already
  says what each undescribed body must be; the same discipline is owed to
  what each mind must be, and that note does not exist yet. **Confirm, and
  say whether a mind-design note is wanted before drafting.**

### Three shapes, put as choices

1. **A new general-tier thread, Season 10, one species.** The fullest
   reading. Costs a registry entry and a tier placement now, and a face if an
   edition follows. Needs a species whose body and society are settled
   enough to carry a season — the Krenyi, the Prismeri or the Pandoids by the
   measure above — and a protagonist set that does not yet exist (character
   pages draft-and-stop).
2. **An alien strand inside the next season of an existing thread.** The
   cheapest first step and the soonest alien-viewpoint prose: a Five-O season
   with an alien strand converging on the task force's case, or the
   "genuinely independent second strand" that `story-bible-summary.md`
   explicitly leaves open for a future S3/S5-position season of the
   Tissadelle arc. Story bible only, no engine change, and it exercises the
   strand pattern a second time.
3. **Both in sequence.** The strand first as proof of voice and of the
   species' mind on the page; promotion to a thread when there is enough for
   a season — the same logic the alien edition's third shape already uses,
   applied one level down.

No recommendation marked as a ruling, but a lean recorded: the second or
third gets an alien ensemble onto the page soonest with nothing to unwind if
the voice does not hold, and the first can be taken later from either.

**Left open — indexed in `open-questions.md`:** the level (thread or strand);
the species, and one or several; the tier if not general; the slot (Season 10,
or which existing season and which human strand it converges with); who the
protagonists are; whether the alien edition follows; whether a mind-design
note is wanted first.

## Sixth, same session — comments on the children's tier

Dermot, verbatim, in two messages:

> should I allow or enable giscus comments on the child reader tier?

> the contemplative tiers use church-space comments, the young adult and
> general tiers share the same comments pool

A question, answered in chat as an assessment; recorded because the answer
is a live-domain change if he wants one. The second message states the
comments architecture, and the registry agrees with it exactly
(`lib/editions.js`, `src/_data/giscus.js`): the three contemplative editions
carry `giscusProfile: "church-space"` (the Communion's board,
`Star-Rangers/churchspace-site-comments`); every other edition falls through
to the default profile (`Star-Rangers/sciencefiction-site-comments`), so the
young-adult and general tiers share one pool.

### What the registry does today, measured

**The children's tier is already in the shared pool, with comments on.** The
`pets` entry sets neither `commentsEnabled` nor `giscusProfile`, so it
inherits the default edition's `commentsEnabled: true` and the default board.
Two consequences follow from how giscus maps discussions: a chapter's thread
is keyed by its permanent `comment_id`, so the discussion under a Season 2
chapter on undercover-pets.com **is the same discussion** as under that
chapter on fianilchruinne.com or starquest.site — adult readers' comments on
the pet chapters appear on the children's site, and vice versa. Non-chapter
pages map by pathname, which is the same across domains too. Nothing about
this was decided; it is what the default does when an entry says nothing.

### What bears on the answer

- **Posting needs a GitHub account.** giscus signs a commenter in through
  GitHub, whose terms require a user to be at least 13; Ireland's digital age
  of consent under GDPR is 16. A child in the tier's readership cannot post,
  and should not be invited to. What the widget offers a child is a place to
  *read* an adult discussion under a story written for them.
- **A separate children's board does not change that.** A new profile and
  repo would isolate the pool (a parent or teacher could post there), at the
  cost of a second board to watch, and the reader profile's rule that the
  children's domain carries no cross-domain link a child cannot judge
  (`edition-reader-profiles.md`, `excludedNotice`) points the same way for a
  comment box.
- **Turning it off costs nothing that exists.** The pets edition has no
  standing discussion of its own to lose; `commentsEnabled: false` is one
  line in the `pets` entry, the shape the sample template already shows.

### Three shapes, put as choices

1. **Off on the children's tier** — `commentsEnabled: false` on the `pets`
   entry. The widget disappears from undercover-pets.com; the same chapters
   keep their discussions on every other domain. **Recommended.**
2. **A children's board of its own** — a third giscus profile and repo,
   isolated from the adult pool. Keeps a comment box for parents and
   teachers; adds a board to moderate; still cannot be used by the reader it
   is for.
3. **Leave it** — shared pool, comments on, as it stands today by default
   rather than by decision.

**Left open — indexed in `open-questions.md`:** which shape. An edition entry
is draft-and-stop, so nothing changed.
