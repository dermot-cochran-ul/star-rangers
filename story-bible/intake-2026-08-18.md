## 1. The holodeck is refused

> *"Added 3D virtual reality holodecks to the list of tropes that I reject."*

**Recorded in `src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md`** as a closing subsection of
*The Furniture of the Future*, plus a summary-table row.

**Canon check changed the drafting, and this is the part to remember.** A first
draft said no room in the record generates a walk-in simulated environment, in
any era. That contradicts `src/glossary/virtual-reality.md`, which holds that
immersive entertainment *"did, once, and the audience left"* — peaked and
declined before 2826 — that the Concord **did not ban** it, and that a Survey
Corps navigator spends real hours inside a visualization rig. So the refusal is
narrowed to the walk-in room and **simulation stands**.

Three arguments, the middle one load-bearing:

- It inherits the hologram's missing medium and needs it everywhere at once,
  plus matter where there is none. A rig makes no such claim: it is a machine
  you are wearing or sitting in, and it knows it.
- **Provenance, not fidelity.** A rig leaves the memory sourced — *four hours on
  the rig*, never confused with the corridor. An indistinguishable room
  manufactures something a person genuinely saw and cannot source, which an
  institution built around an Archive cannot keep in the corner.
- The kernel refuses the cast: a simulated person who answers back and does not
  say what it is, is the earpiece pretending to be an alien, given a body.

## 2. Virtual reality has exactly one live use

> *"In general virtual reality is not used as technology except in one sense — as
> a way of visualizing and understanding complex sensor readings including alien
> communications and signals."*

**This was already canon in embryo and is now generalised.**
`virtual-reality.md` already carried a section headed *The Exception That Isn't
One*, naming fold-scaffold visualization as the field's *"one enduring,
unremarked-upon use"* and describing it exactly right: *"the scaffold's own
topology, **translated rather than invented**."*

What the direction adds is scope: the exception is **a class, not an instance**.
The rig's subject is any measured structure a baseline sense cannot reach —
boundary-zone phase families, low-frequency recurrence, a return with more
dimensions than a person can hold at once — and **alien communication is now
named as the demanding case and the one it is most valued for**.

**The hard boundary is recorded with it, because the two rules meet exactly.**
A rig renders **structure**; it does not deliver **meaning**. It shows a
xenolinguist that a figure recurs at an interval and breaks in a place; it never
says what the figure means and never says anything in a voice. That remains
years of translation work by named people with error bars — which is where *the
universal translator is refused* begins. Seeing a pattern and understanding it
are two jobs, and the machine is only qualified for the first.

Note the entry's own line that does useful work elsewhere: Chthonari engineers
and Prismeri astrogators read comparable structure through other senses, so
**baseline names a sense, never a standard**.

## 3. In-universe fiction has the structure of a structured record

> *"Works of in-universe fiction have a similar structure to the star-rangers
> repo with more audiovisual content and adaptations for alien modes of
> communication and vice versa when the author is a sapient alien."*

**Drafted as new lore:** `src/lore/narrative-works-and-their-adaptation.md`.

**The craft constraint applied, and worth keeping for anything that touches
this:** the entry describes the form entirely from inside and **never references
this site, this repository, or the resemblance**. A wink would cost more than it
pays, and the resemblance is the reader's to notice or not. Nothing in the entry
is meta.

What it commits the setting to:

- **A work is a spine of scenes, each carrying several viewpoints of the same
  events**, set down separately and not reconciled by the work itself. Readers
  move through it in more than one order and works are indexed for it.
- **An apparatus is published with it** — settled background, a register of
  terms, a chronology, and attributed source documents that may be partial or
  self-serving. *"A work whose documents all agree with each other is generally
  suspected of not having been thought about very hard."*
- **Three in-world causes**, none artistic in origin: a documentary culture that
  learned to ask who says so; the failure of the single credible narrator, taken
  from the archive's own hardest cases; and **modularity for adaptation**, which
  is the pressure that shows most.
- **Audiovisual, but not cinema.** Imagery on matte unlit surfaces read by
  ambient light, nothing glowing, nothing projected; sound present, not theatre.
  *"Closer to a scored and illustrated record than to a film."* An image is
  evidence about a viewpoint, never a fact about the room.
- **Adaptation is years of named human work**, because there is no translating
  machine. The Chthonari case is the taught one: a human work entering the
  Undersong is re-composed into a channel where the separation between a
  character's thought and the work's telling of it *does not exist*. The reverse
  is not symmetrical — a human reader receives an account.
- **An adapter may decline**, on the Ollune precedent, and the refusal is
  recorded rather than worked around.
- **The original is the work; the adaptation is the courtesy** — attributed,
  dated, revisable, never mistaken for the thing itself. Serious works
  accumulate several, by different hands, disagreeing.
- **Leaving something unrendered is a statement**, read the way a people keeping
  its own calendar is read.

### Live after this

- **The Survey Archive has no lore page.** It is named across journal entries and
  in codex `library:` fields, and a draft link to
  `/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-survey-archive/` was invented and caught by
  `check-internal-links.js`. Whether the Archive should have its own entry is a
  real question and is not answered here; the draft now points at the
  verification doctrine and marked-absences codex entries instead.
- **No in-universe work is named.** The entry describes the form and cites no
  title, which keeps it from committing the setting to a specific canonical
  novel. Naming one is a separate decision and a good hook.
- **Adaptation status versus the four canon statuses.** An adaptation is filed
  as an attributed account, which places it very close to Codex ground without
  the entry saying so. Worth settling whether an in-universe adaptation of an
  in-universe work is *codex-shaped* in the taxonomy or simply outside it.

## 4. The Krenyi have little appetite for entertainment

> *"Krenyi have little interest in fiction, games, team sports, music or other
> forms of entertainment that humans enjoy."*

**Drafted into `src/lore/krenyi.md`** as a new section, plus one bullet added to
the existing *Present Tense* consequence list.

**Derived, not asserted.** The page already carries the mechanism: the Krenyi
orientation is to what is here and coming, and *"the test is not is it true but
**does it still do work**."* The section is written as one more thing falling out
of that orientation, using the page's own established move — the demisexuality
paragraph, which explains three listed traits as one thing rather than three.

- **Fiction** asks attention for people not present and events that did not
  occur: the past-as-explanation problem in its most voluntary form, and
  mythification undertaken on purpose. The characterful detail: a Krenyi will
  read a work's **apparatus** — terms, chronology, sourced documents, which are
  measurements — and put the scenes down without any sense of having missed a
  point.
- **Games and spectator contests** turn on a distinction between a **drill** and
  a **game**: a drill measures what someone can do, a game produces a winner.
  Second and structural reason for the spectator half — its register is dread
  and fury, and the Krenyi are bounded against both, so *"there is nothing
  arriving."*
- **Music is marked, not explained.** The commemorative use is covered
  (commemoration being one of the three deployments of the past they decline),
  and that does not cover the rest. **The absence is theirs, not the archive's**
  — the same construction the homeworld gap already uses on this page.

**Two things deliberately not claimed.** That the Krenyi have no forms of their
own doing this work — *"nobody should assume the answer is none, and nobody has
been told it is not"* — and that this is a deficit. The section ends on the
ladder refusal: a people who want little of what you make are not a people with
less; they are a people the thing was not made for.

**Paired with §3.** `narrative-works-and-their-adaptation.md` now names the
Krenyi as the instructive absence in the adaptation economy: the people nearest
to human in build want least of what humans make, and there is no significant
body of adaptation into Krenyi use because the demand never formed. *"The hard
part was never the sensorium."*

### Reading confirmed, and the draft re-derived

**Dermot, same day: reading (b) — *little interest in the human forms of them*.**

This mattered more than it looks. The first draft derived the disinterest from
the general orientation — these things *"return nothing usable"* — and that
derivation is **too strong under (b)**, because it applies to any such form
including a Krenyi one, and so quietly predicts they have no equivalents. The
section was rewritten to explain why the **human** forms specifically fail:

- **A calibration problem, not a verdict on made things.** *"Human entertainment
  is tuned, with great skill, to an instrument the Krenyi do not have."*
- **Three registers they are structurally without.** Human drama runs on dread
  and its release, sport on partisan fury and the fear of losing, much music on
  holding a sorrow open. Deep fear, fury and unresolving grief are exactly the
  three bounds already established under Emotional Range and Resistance to
  Grief-Predation. *"The performance is reaching for handholds that are not
  there."*
- **Fiction's method is the one they decline everywhere else** — taking an
  invented account as an account, and reading people as symbol and pattern.
- **Team sport asks a person to become a category on purpose**, the single move
  the Quiet-Built are built to refuse. They will compete, train and test; it is
  *"the stands rather than the field where nothing arrives."*

**What this leaves open, now pointedly.** Whether the Krenyi have forms of their
own is still unestablished, and under (b) it becomes the interesting question
rather than a loose end: what a made thing calibrated to Krenyi registers would
look like — no dread, no fury, grief that finishes, a courtesy in the present
tense — has never been recorded cross-species. The absence stays **theirs, not
the archive's**, and the section says so. Answering it is a further canon
decision and a good one.

**And the paired entry gained its real point from this.** Under (b) the
`narrative-works` paragraph sharpens from *they want less* to something better:
the species sharing almost the whole human body plan is the one human stories
reach least, while peoples built nothing like us — adapted for at enormous cost
— want the work done. *"What a story is for does not travel with the shape of
the body reading it."*

## 5. Krenyi, Levrils, and Champions

> *"Krenyi are the beings with the least awe and reverence towards Levrils, yet
> also the most obedient, cooperative and responsive to subtle Levrilic
> influence, making them the first to act as champions and heroes."*

**Drafted into `src/lore/krenyi.md`** as two new sections, placed with the other
resistances since it is the same faculty producing the opposite of what anyone
expects.

**The paradox resolves on one line:** *awe and attention compete for the same
faculty, and the Krenyi have spent it all on attention.* Everything else follows
from traits already on the page, so nothing new was invented:

- **Least awe** is resistance to mythification meeting its largest available
  case. A Levril arriving is, for most witnesses, first an event *about
  themselves*. A Krenyi does none of that, so what is left once the glamour
  finds no purchase is the material part.
- **Most responsive** because **subtle influence has to be noticed to be
  answered**. Levrilic action is a sculpted gradient rather than an altered
  outcome, and a witness occupied with being amazed is not reading it. The
  Quiet-Built read **pattern drift** professionally — the same instrument tilted
  at a different object. *"The clarity that refuses the glamour is the clarity
  that catches the gradient. They are not more obedient than other peoples. They
  are earlier, because nothing happens in them first."*
- **Readiest Champions** because selection is a bounded assessment on constraint
  literacy and causal relevance — sustained competent action under constraint,
  observed rather than tested — which a people immune to the panic cascade and
  content to spend centuries becoming precise produce at an unmatched rate.

**"Obedient" was deliberately not rendered as deference**, and a floor is
stated. Krenyi extend good faith first, to a Levril as to anyone, and nothing
compels them — they are unenslavable in the cosmology's strict sense, and Cascade
standing is not a lever. A Levril whose dealings drifted from their stated terms
would meet what anyone would: the drift registers, relevance is withdrawn
quietly. *"Their cooperation is not owed and is not obtained. It is extended, and
it can be spent."* Without this the direction would have made them tools.

**The beat the section is built to land.** The documented aftermath of Champion
selection is *"frequent mythic overstatement of actual capability"* and
*"persistent misattribution as divine will by low-context observers."* So **the
people least willing to be read as symbol are the people most often selected, and
therefore the most mythologised.** They can name the distortion in the room and
have never found a way to name it in the story told afterwards, in a settlement
they have left, by people who were not there. Their material answer is read
invariably as modesty; it is the same refusal they make about a homeworld, and it
leaves the same space that other peoples have never once left empty.

### Live after this

- **"First" was read as readiest rather than historically first.** The section
  says *"being first is not zeal — it is the absence of a delay everyone else
  has."* If historical primacy was meant — that the earliest Champions on record
  were Krenyi — that is a different and larger claim, and it is not drafted.
- **Arilon.** `arilon.md` holds that Arilon-pattern accounts are the recorded
  footprint of Champions across a hundred traditions, each certain it was the
  only community visited. If the Krenyi are disproportionately selected, some of
  those accounts plausibly concern a people who look *almost* human — which is a
  genuinely interesting implication and is **not asserted anywhere**. Worth
  deciding deliberately rather than discovering mid-draft.
- **`levril.md` was not edited.** The glossary states selection criteria without
  naming any population as disproportionately meeting them. A one-line pointer
  would help discoverability and is a second published-lore edit; not taken.

## 6. The Arilon implication, drafted

Dermot asked for the §5 consequence to be drafted as a proposal. Done, in
`src/lore/arilon.md`, with a reciprocal pointer from the Krenyi Champions
section.

**Filed as a refinement *inside* the Champion reading, not a fourth peer to it.**
This is the decision that keeps it from disturbing anything. The Avatar exception
sits *above* the Champion reading (a different stratum); the Krenyi reading sits
*within* it (a Champion **of which people?**). So `arilonian.md`'s three-way list
— Champion, avatar, or incarnation of Kieme — stays correct and needed no edit.

**Why it fits, in the pattern's own words.** Arilon is *"an outsider who arrives"*,
names what local authority will not, is opposed, and departs before vindication.
The Krenyi: the only other people on the human frame, so they pass as a person to
a low-context observer; long-lived without apparent degradation, so they do not
age on a community's timescale; straight to material terms; honest, so they say
the thing everyone present has agreed not to say; decline to be placed by a
homeworld; and withdraw relevance quietly and leave. Plus readiest Champions on
the Cascade's own criteria.

**Why it is permanent rather than merely plausible.** No filed homeworld, no
emergence date, no timeline placement — so there is no chronology to test any
account against. The consequence is stated as theirs and unchosen: *"a people who
decline to be placed cannot be placed anywhere, including out of an account they
were never in."* And they will not settle it: asked, a Krenyi gives the material
answer and declines the rest, on the same grounds as every *what are you, really*.

**Two guards written in, because this reading is more dangerous than it looks.**

- **It is the deflationary one** — it lets a sceptic swap a visitation for a
  long-lived traveller and go home satisfied — and it is no better evidenced than
  the devotional accounts it would displace. The entry says preferring it because
  it is tidier is *"the same error the file spends its whole existence declining,
  running in the other direction,"* and cites the Corps maxim **the pleasing
  reading is checked first** (verified merged on `main`).
- **Nothing is adjudicated.** The Fellowship files it exactly as it files the
  Avatar exception, and the entry adopts nothing.

**The closing beat.** The Quiet-Built would refuse this reading if refusing were
available: to be read as symbol is what they resist everywhere, and to be read as
*the source of everyone else's symbols* is that distortion at the largest scale
the record offers — with no material term to return the exchange to, because the
exchange is being conducted across a hundred traditions by people who were not
there.

### Corrected the same day: the pattern is species-neutral

> *"Arilon patterns could apply to sapient beings of any kind, not just
> humanoids."*

**This corrected the draft, which had leaned on the Krenyi passing as human and
so quietly made humanoid-ness central to the pattern.** Two changes:

- **Stated outright in the Cascade section.** A Champion is a *sentient material
  being — organic or artificial*, and nothing in the selection criteria prefers a
  humanoid, a biological, or a speaker. An Arilon-figure may be of any sapient
  kind, and so may the community that received one. The file is a comparative
  archive of **peoples**.
- **The Krenyi paragraph was re-argued.** Their resemblance to humans is no
  longer offered as *why the reading is strong* — that gets the logic backwards.
  It bears on the **archive**, not on frequency: a Krenyi Champion among humans is
  a case that would have been written down as a *person* and so preserved in the
  pattern's recognisable shape.

**And the correction produced the better idea, which is the selection effect.**
What is not species-neutral is the shelf. The filed pattern — a teacher, a
witness, a wanderer — describes what survived transmission by low-context
observers using the vocabulary they had. A Champion who looked broadly like the
people visited is written down as a person; one who did not may be written down
as a marvel, a beast, a voice, a light — and such an account is less likely to be
recognised as the same pattern, less likely to be cross-referenced, and in some
traditions less likely to have been kept. **The accounts on the shelf are not the
accounts that exist. They are the accounts that looked like accounts.**

That is deliberately the [boundary-zone](../src/lore/boundary-zones.md) record's
own move — *"the two sites on the books are not the two that exist; they are the
two that could be stood on"* — and the entry says the Fellowship borrowed the
phrasing knowingly.

The guard follows from it: read carelessly, the Krenyi over-representation
becomes evidence that Champions are usually Krenyi, which is exactly the
inference the annotation exists to forbid.

### Live after this

- **Old Earth.** The pattern includes Old-Earth accounts (the Jehu comparandum),
  so the reading implies unrecorded Krenyi presence in antiquity. The entry does
  **not** assert presence — only that the record cannot exclude it, and says why.
  Whether Krenyi were ever on Old Earth is a genuine canon question and is not
  answered here.
- **Syra.** A Krenyi is a present-day POV character. Nothing in the draft touches
  her, and no chapter is affected, but any future scene where someone puts this
  reading to a Krenyi now has a canonical answer available: the material one,
  and the decline.

## 7. Krenyi understand human emotions without sharing them

> *"Krenyi, unlike the Vulcans of Star Trek, can understand human emotions
> without sharing them."*

**Drafted into the Emotional Range section of `krenyi.md`**, directly after the
existing *"This is not suppression. It is structural."*

**The comparison is not named in the entry**, and should not be — the record
names tropes generically and never franchises. What the entry does instead is
foreclose the reading by stating what is true.

**It derives, and the mechanism was already canon.** [What the Record
Refuses](../src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md) re-derives empathy as *"intuition
coupled with high sensitivity … It is cognition, which is why it is trainable"*,
and refuses the antenna outright — *"nothing arrives by antenna; everything
arrives by attention."* So **understanding never required sharing, for anyone**.
A Krenyi reading a frightened human is not compensating for a missing sense; they
are using the only mechanism this record gives anybody.

Four things the passage now says:

- **They are not an unemotional people.** Anxiety, annoyance and grief they have;
  attachments of genuine care they hold for centuries. The range is **bounded at
  three points** — deep fear, fury, grief that will not close — and missing three
  extremes is not missing the register. *"Not calm the way a held breath is calm.
  Calm the way a room is quiet when nothing in it is on fire."*
- **Comprehension without sharing**, stated plainly: what the fear is doing to
  that person's judgement, what it will make them say, what they will regret.
- **One specific advantage, carefully bounded.** A human reads another's fear
  *through their own* — the instrument is measuring something it is also doing —
  and a Krenyi's is not. That is the same fact as their effectiveness where panic
  cascades are a concern. Explicitly **not a rank**: a different instrument, not a
  better grade of the same one, per the ladder refusal.
- **The cost, and it lands where a reader might not look.** Human emotional
  grammar is *learned*, so what they carry is a very good model — and a model is
  what a novel case defeats, which is exactly why the page already records them
  as briefly vulnerable to unfamiliar human deceptions. *"Working from a map where
  a human is working from the ground."*

**Closing guard:** clarity is not logic — *"logic is a method and clarity is an
absence of ornament"* — and a Krenyi has never been recorded finding a human
ridiculous for being afraid. That last is the sentence doing the real work
against the stock reading.

**Why this mattered now.** §4's entertainment section is exactly the passage that
could invite the wrong inference — a people unmoved by human drama read as a
people who do not understand it. They are the opposite: they read it accurately
and are not moved, which are two independent facts.

### Available, not taken

A trope row in `what-the-record-refuses.md` for **the emotionless logical alien**
would fit that page's existing shape, beside the re-derived empath. Not drafted,
because the direction named the Krenyi specifically and a second lore file is a
separate proposal.

## 8. Were the Krenyi ever on Old Earth? — recommended NO

Dermot asked for a ruling. **This is a recommendation and a draft, not a
decision taken** — a canon assertion of this reach is his, and the opposite
ruling is available and would take one paragraph to write.

**Recommended: no. The Krenyi reading stops at Old Earth.**

### What the check found

- **Nothing in the record places any people but humans on Earth before
  spaceflight.** `grep` across lore for pre-spaceflight non-human presence
  returns nothing.
- **Contact history is uniformly late.** Federation first contact came decades
  after the 2723 charter; the Celtic Union was re-contacted *"within the last
  generation."* Humanity's alien contact is a post-fold phenomenon throughout.
- **The Old-Earth Arilon comparanda are scriptural** — the Elijah/Jehu pair,
  carried off Earth as living observance.

### The two reasons, and the second generalises

1. **Evidential.** An unplaceable people is not a placeable one. That the Krenyi
   have no filed homeworld makes the reading impossible to *disprove*, and
   impossible to disprove is not supported. **This corrects a subtle flaw in the
   merged draft**, which framed non-placement as making the reading permanent
   without noting that the same fact denies it any positive support. The absence
   of a chronology is not a licence to write them into every century lacking one.
2. **A rule the record keeps everywhere: no people is the explanation for another
   people's history.** It is the refusal underneath the closed list of
   human-framed peoples and the refusal of a shared galactic biochemistry — each
   declining a tidier universe where everything descends from one source.
   Supplying an alien visitor behind Old-Earth accounts would tidy several
   thousand years of somebody else's history into a footnote about the
   Quiet-Built, on material living traditions hold sacred and the Fellowship
   exists to hold *jointly rather than to settle*. **A file whose founding
   renunciation is that it does not adjudicate cannot hand one tradition a
   mechanical answer and call it neutrality.**

### What survives, and why the ruling improves the entry

The Krenyi reading is not killed — it is **bounded to where it was always
interesting**: the pattern's later accounts, across star systems, in the contact
era that produced most of the file. And the deflationary guard now has teeth,
because the first place a careless reader reaches — ancient Earth — is exactly
where the reading is ruled not to go.

### If the opposite is wanted

A yes would need to carry, deliberately: unrecorded Krenyi presence in human
antiquity; the secret-history trope the record otherwise refuses in spirit; and a
mechanical explanation offered for accounts the Fellowship holds jointly. Each is
writable. None is currently written, and none should arrive as a side effect of
this reading rather than as its own decision.

## 9. The Umbral Moon fix, drafted — `src/lore/umbral-moon.md`

Dermot asked for the #462 fix to be drafted. The resolution is recorded in place
in `intake-2026-07-26.md` block 2; the entry is drafted on it.

**Two of #462's three problems were overstated by me, and the checks said so.**

- **The neighbour membrane never needed naming.** #462 called it a blocker —
  *"cannot be written until the neighbour membrane is named"*. It is not: the
  record **never names the far side** at its two documented boundary zones,
  saying only *"a neighbour"* throughout. So the fix is to **drop** the Shadow
  Universe rather than replace it, and **the coupling to *where shadow beings
  live* is broken** — that question stays open on its own merits, and is no
  longer holding a world hostage.
- **Shadow beings needed no home at all.** They are meta-dimensional — extending
  across higher spatial dimensions rather than residing in a universe next door —
  so the realm question is the wrong shape. The entry says so and keeps the
  register: *"a place where the local physics is thin enough that their presence
  registers."*

**The one real decision — what holds the moon — resolves by separating two
questions the block had merged.** *Why is it there* and *why is the place strange*
are different, and answering them separately removes the contradiction with no new
mechanism: the moon is a **Trojan at Earth–Moon L5**, held by ordinary Lagrange
dynamics, and the flexure merely shares the address. The entry states plainly that
no gravity tunnel could hold anything, since one lasts seconds to minutes and is
*"not stable enough to hold open on purpose."*

**What survives from the block, improved.** The detection story: the *"anomalous
gravitational micro-pulses"* are the flexure rather than the moon — far-side mass
registering intermittently as the Interval varies — and the moon turns up in the
follow-up *"having nothing to do with it."* New London's logged disturbances become
the Archive's standing complaint: the readings were correct throughout and nobody
had asked the question they were answers to.

### Live after this

- **Coincidence.** The entry has the flexure and L5 sharing coordinates and says
  the coincidence *"is doing no work in any account that needs it."* That is a
  deliberate refusal to make it meaningful. If it should mean something, that is a
  decision and a good one — but it should be made rather than inherited.
- **Nothing else about the moon is described** — no composition, no history, no
  visitors' record. The entry is about the site, and deliberately stops there.
