## 1. The holodeck is refused

> *"Added 3D virtual reality holodecks to the list of tropes that I reject."*

**Recorded in `src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md`** as a closing subsection of
*The Furniture of the Future*, plus a summary-table row.

**Canon check changed the drafting, and this is the part to remember.** A first
draft said no room in the record generates a walk-in simulated environment, in
any era. That contradicts `src/glossary/virtual-reality.md`, which holds that
immersive entertainment *"did, once, and the audience left"* — peaked and
declined before 2826 — that the Concord **did not ban** it, and that a Survey
Corps navigator spends real hours inside a visualization rig. So the refusal is
narrowed to the walk-in room and **simulation stands**.

Three arguments, the middle one load-bearing:

- It inherits the hologram's missing medium and needs it everywhere at once,
  plus matter where there is none. A rig makes no such claim: it is a machine
  you are wearing or sitting in, and it knows it.
- **Provenance, not fidelity.** A rig leaves the memory sourced — *four hours on
  the rig*, never confused with the corridor. An indistinguishable room
  manufactures something a person genuinely saw and cannot source, which an
  institution built around an Archive cannot keep in the corner.
- The kernel refuses the cast: a simulated person who answers back and does not
  say what it is, is the earpiece pretending to be an alien, given a body.

## 2. Virtual reality has exactly one live use

> *"In general virtual reality is not used as technology except in one sense — as
> a way of visualizing and understanding complex sensor readings including alien
> communications and signals."*

**This was already canon in embryo and is now generalised.**
`virtual-reality.md` already carried a section headed *The Exception That Isn't
One*, naming fold-scaffold visualization as the field's *"one enduring,
unremarked-upon use"* and describing it exactly right: *"the scaffold's own
topology, **translated rather than invented**."*

What the direction adds is scope: the exception is **a class, not an instance**.
The rig's subject is any measured structure a baseline sense cannot reach —
boundary-zone phase families, low-frequency recurrence, a return with more
dimensions than a person can hold at once — and **alien communication is now
named as the demanding case and the one it is most valued for**.

**The hard boundary is recorded with it, because the two rules meet exactly.**
A rig renders **structure**; it does not deliver **meaning**. It shows a
xenolinguist that a figure recurs at an interval and breaks in a place; it never
says what the figure means and never says anything in a voice. That remains
years of translation work by named people with error bars — which is where *the
universal translator is refused* begins. Seeing a pattern and understanding it
are two jobs, and the machine is only qualified for the first.

Note the entry's own line that does useful work elsewhere: Chthonari engineers
and Prismeri astrogators read comparable structure through other senses, so
**baseline names a sense, never a standard**.

## 3. In-universe fiction has the structure of a structured record

> *"Works of in-universe fiction have a similar structure to the star-rangers
> repo with more audiovisual content and adaptations for alien modes of
> communication and vice versa when the author is a sapient alien."*

**Drafted as new lore:** `src/lore/narrative-works-and-their-adaptation.md`.

**The craft constraint applied, and worth keeping for anything that touches
this:** the entry describes the form entirely from inside and **never references
this site, this repository, or the resemblance**. A wink would cost more than it
pays, and the resemblance is the reader's to notice or not. Nothing in the entry
is meta.

What it commits the setting to:

- **A work is a spine of scenes, each carrying several viewpoints of the same
  events**, set down separately and not reconciled by the work itself. Readers
  move through it in more than one order and works are indexed for it.
- **An apparatus is published with it** — settled background, a register of
  terms, a chronology, and attributed source documents that may be partial or
  self-serving. *"A work whose documents all agree with each other is generally
  suspected of not having been thought about very hard."*
- **Three in-world causes**, none artistic in origin: a documentary culture that
  learned to ask who says so; the failure of the single credible narrator, taken
  from the archive's own hardest cases; and **modularity for adaptation**, which
  is the pressure that shows most.
- **Audiovisual, but not cinema.** Imagery on matte unlit surfaces read by
  ambient light, nothing glowing, nothing projected; sound present, not theatre.
  *"Closer to a scored and illustrated record than to a film."* An image is
  evidence about a viewpoint, never a fact about the room.
- **Adaptation is years of named human work**, because there is no translating
  machine. The Chthonari case is the taught one: a human work entering the
  Undersong is re-composed into a channel where the separation between a
  character's thought and the work's telling of it *does not exist*. The reverse
  is not symmetrical — a human reader receives an account.
- **An adapter may decline**, on the Ollune precedent, and the refusal is
  recorded rather than worked around.
- **The original is the work; the adaptation is the courtesy** — attributed,
  dated, revisable, never mistaken for the thing itself. Serious works
  accumulate several, by different hands, disagreeing.
- **Leaving something unrendered is a statement**, read the way a people keeping
  its own calendar is read.

### Live after this

- **The Survey Archive has no lore page.** It is named across journal entries and
  in codex `library:` fields, and a draft link to
  `/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-survey-archive/` was invented and caught by
  `check-internal-links.js`. Whether the Archive should have its own entry is a
  real question and is not answered here; the draft now points at the
  verification doctrine and marked-absences codex entries instead.
- **No in-universe work is named.** The entry describes the form and cites no
  title, which keeps it from committing the setting to a specific canonical
  novel. Naming one is a separate decision and a good hook.
- **Adaptation status versus the four canon statuses.** An adaptation is filed
  as an attributed account, which places it very close to Codex ground without
  the entry saying so. Worth settling whether an in-universe adaptation of an
  in-universe work is *codex-shaped* in the taxonomy or simply outside it.

## 4. The Krenyi have little appetite for entertainment

> *"Krenyi have little interest in fiction, games, team sports, music or other
> forms of entertainment that humans enjoy."*

**Drafted into `src/lore/krenyi.md`** as a new section, plus one bullet added to
the existing *Present Tense* consequence list.

**Derived, not asserted.** The page already carries the mechanism: the Krenyi
orientation is to what is here and coming, and *"the test is not is it true but
**does it still do work**."* The section is written as one more thing falling out
of that orientation, using the page's own established move — the demisexuality
paragraph, which explains three listed traits as one thing rather than three.

- **Fiction** asks attention for people not present and events that did not
  occur: the past-as-explanation problem in its most voluntary form, and
  mythification undertaken on purpose. The characterful detail: a Krenyi will
  read a work's **apparatus** — terms, chronology, sourced documents, which are
  measurements — and put the scenes down without any sense of having missed a
  point.
- **Games and spectator contests** turn on a distinction between a **drill** and
  a **game**: a drill measures what someone can do, a game produces a winner.
  Second and structural reason for the spectator half — its register is dread
  and fury, and the Krenyi are bounded against both, so *"there is nothing
  arriving."*
- **Music is marked, not explained.** The commemorative use is covered
  (commemoration being one of the three deployments of the past they decline),
  and that does not cover the rest. **The absence is theirs, not the archive's**
  — the same construction the homeworld gap already uses on this page.

**Two things deliberately not claimed.** That the Krenyi have no forms of their
own doing this work — *"nobody should assume the answer is none, and nobody has
been told it is not"* — and that this is a deficit. The section ends on the
ladder refusal: a people who want little of what you make are not a people with
less; they are a people the thing was not made for.

**Paired with §3.** `narrative-works-and-their-adaptation.md` now names the
Krenyi as the instructive absence in the adaptation economy: the people nearest
to human in build want least of what humans make, and there is no significant
body of adaptation into Krenyi use because the demand never formed. *"The hard
part was never the sensorium."*

### Reading confirmed, and the draft re-derived

**Dermot, same day: reading (b) — *little interest in the human forms of them*.**

This mattered more than it looks. The first draft derived the disinterest from
the general orientation — these things *"return nothing usable"* — and that
derivation is **too strong under (b)**, because it applies to any such form
including a Krenyi one, and so quietly predicts they have no equivalents. The
section was rewritten to explain why the **human** forms specifically fail:

- **A calibration problem, not a verdict on made things.** *"Human entertainment
  is tuned, with great skill, to an instrument the Krenyi do not have."*
- **Three registers they are structurally without.** Human drama runs on dread
  and its release, sport on partisan fury and the fear of losing, much music on
  holding a sorrow open. Deep fear, fury and unresolving grief are exactly the
  three bounds already established under Emotional Range and Resistance to
  Grief-Predation. *"The performance is reaching for handholds that are not
  there."*
- **Fiction's method is the one they decline everywhere else** — taking an
  invented account as an account, and reading people as symbol and pattern.
- **Team sport asks a person to become a category on purpose**, the single move
  the Quiet-Built are built to refuse. They will compete, train and test; it is
  *"the stands rather than the field where nothing arrives."*

**What this leaves open, now pointedly.** Whether the Krenyi have forms of their
own is still unestablished, and under (b) it becomes the interesting question
rather than a loose end: what a made thing calibrated to Krenyi registers would
look like — no dread, no fury, grief that finishes, a courtesy in the present
tense — has never been recorded cross-species. The absence stays **theirs, not
the archive's**, and the section says so. Answering it is a further canon
decision and a good one.

**And the paired entry gained its real point from this.** Under (b) the
`narrative-works` paragraph sharpens from *they want less* to something better:
the species sharing almost the whole human body plan is the one human stories
reach least, while peoples built nothing like us — adapted for at enormous cost
— want the work done. *"What a story is for does not travel with the shape of
the body reading it."*
