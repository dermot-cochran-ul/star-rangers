## 1. Galahad and Elvira are the main romance, "almost forgotten about"

**Verified, and he is right — it is not written.** A repo-wide search for
romance vocabulary (*romance, betrothed, sweetheart, lover*, and similar)
returns **zero matches in `src/` and `story-bible/`**. The relationship exists
as exactly one line, on Elvira's page:

> She pays attention to vocabulary. When Galahad uses precise language for the
> boundary events — not fumbling for metaphors, not reaching for folklore — she
> notices immediately, and **revises her model of him upward**.

That is a good first beat and it is the only beat. For a thread described as the
main romance of the series, one sentence of upward revision is the whole record.

**Worth preserving when it gets written:** the attraction as established is
*competence-first and vocabulary-specific*. She notices him because he refuses
imprecision. That is unusual, in keeping with both characters, and much better
than whatever a generic version would supply. Build from it rather than past it.

**Complication to resolve first.** Elvira's classification is a live, unresolved
story question — her own page says she is "clearly not human in the standard
sense" and that her species and origin "are not resolved by external insertion —
only by story events." A romance thread will apply pressure to that question,
because a relationship eventually asks what she is. That is an opportunity, not
an obstacle, but it means the romance cannot be written in isolation from the
Open Thread on her page.

### Naming: settled — she is Elvira

Dermot wrote "Elvia" when raising this; confirmed 28 July 2026 that **Elvira**
is what he meant. No rename, nothing to change. The repo is already correct
throughout — `src/characters/elvira.md`, `id: elvira`, referenced from
`aldera.md` and the Season 1 Episode 0 prequel.

## 2. Asteria mentored Galahad during his exile years

**Asteria fits well.** `src/characters/asteria-the-sage.md` has her as a retired
Star Captain who withdrew to a quiet planet and leads its Fellowship of Light
chapter — precisely the figure a disgraced or displaced Survey Corps officer
would end up in the orbit of, and it gives her a function in the story beyond
being quotable about the Cascade.

**No conflict with Elvira's household sage.** Elvira's Dún Rí years were
"guided by the household sage" (unnamed, `elvira.md`). Asteria is not that
person and should not be quietly merged into her; two different mentors,
different worlds, no tidy convergence needed.

### Resolved 28 July 2026: not exile — long frontier missions

Dermot replaced the exile framing with **long frontier missions**, which removes
the problem entirely. Exile appeared nowhere in canon and would have contradicted
his page, which has twelve unbroken Survey Corps years across six prior boundary
stations with no gap in the account. (The only "exile" in the repo belongs to
**Aldera**, the cat who chose the garden gate over the terrace, in the Dún Rí
chronicle and the Episode 0 prequel.)

Long frontier missions cost nothing. They *are* the six prior boundary stations
— the same twelve years, now with a texture: extended postings a long way out,
which is exactly where a man learns to read strata in incident reports and
develops vocabulary his supervisors will not accept without annotation.

**And it explains Asteria.** She "withdrew to a quiet planet after her service."
A quiet planet is where long frontier missions take you. A retired Star Captain
and a surveyor on a distant posting is a plausible, unforced meeting — no exile,
no disgrace, no gap to justify. It also gives their relationship the right
shape: not rescue, just proximity and time.

**The one thing still worth deciding:** what he came back with. A mentorship
should explain something about the Galahad of Season 1 — most obviously his
extended boundary vocabulary, and his habit of knowing precisely when to use the
field term and when to write what a supervisor will accept. If Asteria taught
him the second half of that, she taught him something closer to politics than
to survey work, which is a good and slightly uncomfortable thing for a sage to
have taught.

## 3. Dr. Falkorian — foster cousin of Karla Wender, Chief Science Officer

New character, no occurrences in the repo. **The rank already exists and fits
exactly**, which makes this cheaper to add than it looks.

`src/lore/star-rangers-command-hierarchy.md` establishes **Chief Science
Officer** as the duty title for a Chief on the research track, running a post or
vessel's science section — the same rank tier as **Chief Pilot**, the title
Wender holds at the start of Season 1. See also
`src/lore/star-rangers-science-corps.md`.

So the two of them start the series at **identical rank on different tracks**,
and are family. That is a good structural spine and it is free — no new rank,
no new institution.

**It also fills a real gap.** There are currently no science-track characters on
the roster at all; the only character page mentioning science is Tissadelle's,
in passing. The Science Corps exists in lore with nobody in it.

**Needs from Dermot:**

- **First name, and whether "Dr." is academic or medical.** The distinction
  matters for what section they run.
- **What "foster cousin" means here** — fostering as a Celtic Union social
  institution would tie into Tír na nÓg material already on the map, and would
  be more interesting than an incidental family fact. Is it a formal
  arrangement with obligations, or just how it worked out?
- **Do they diverge or converge?** Wender's competence is judging what is
  executable; a science officer's is judging what is *true*. Those conflict
  productively, and a family relationship means neither can retreat into rank
  when they do.
- **Season 6-7 role, if any.** Rook-7 is the last remaining purely instrumental
  character in that treatment. A Chief Science Officer with a family claim on
  Wender is a candidate to take that weight instead — the person who can tell
  her that her doctrine is wrong and cannot be dismissed for it.

## 4. Daniel Altarei — Star Rangers Chief Engineer who becomes a Detective Inspector

New character. Half of this lands on existing canon and half requires a small
addition.

**Detective Inspector is established.** `src/characters/bertram-ashcombe.md`
holds that exact rank in the Threshold Station Constabulary — immaculate,
cheerful, excellent closure rate, serenely certain the cases solve themselves.
So Altarei's second career is a move into a real institution with a
ready-made colleague, and Ashcombe is an obvious foil: an engineer who
reconstructs a failure from physical evidence against a man who is very good at
this and has never had to explain how.

**Resolved by Dermot, 28 July 2026: he was a Chief in the Engineering Corps** —
the rank, serving in a corps, not a new duty title. That needs nothing added.
My earlier note that "Chief Engineer is not established" was right about the
*duty title* only: the hierarchy's five Chief titles are Chief Ranger, Chief
Pilot, senior chief warden officer, Chief Provost and Chief Science Officer,
with no engineering entry. But the **Engineering Corps itself is well
established**, and richly:

- `src/lore/chthonari.md` — Chthonari are "disproportionately represented in the
  Star Rangers' Engineering Corps, specifically in fold-scaffold construction
  and maintenance", a career choice rather than a species quota, and their
  recruiters are tired of explaining the difference to visiting press.
- `src/lore/federation-of-sentient-beings.md` and
  `src/glossary/virtual-reality.md` both reference it as an existing institution
  with its own culture.

**The one gap:** there are lore files for the Frontier, Safety and Science
Corps but **none for the Engineering Corps** — it exists only inside other
entries. If Altarei is going to carry it, that file is the natural companion
piece, the same note as applies to Dr. Lekrin and the Medical Corps.

**Good material sitting there already.** A human Chief in a corps where
Chthonari dominate the prestige specialization is a specific place to stand, and
the Chthonari entry describes fold-scaffold work as reading "a structure under
gradient stress that must be read and corrected before it fails." That is
forensic reading. The bridge from engineer to Detective Inspector is not a
career change at all — it is the same act performed on a different object, and
the story should probably know that before Altarei does.

**Why the career change is the interesting part.** Both jobs are the same
skill pointed at different objects: reconstructing what happened from what is
left. An engineer reads a failed component; a detective reads a scene. The move
is lateral, not a demotion or a reinvention, and the story should probably know
that before Altarei does.

**Still needs from Dermot:** what ended the engineering career — chosen, or
made for him — and whether he serves at Threshold alongside Ashcombe or
somewhere else.

## 5. Dr. Lekrin — Star Rangers Medical Corps

New character. **Spelling corrected by Dermot mid-session from "Lerkin" to
"Lekrin"** — use Lekrin.

**The Medical Corps is well established** and, unusually, is established
mostly through its *limits*:
`src/lore/post-teleport-ascension-stress-disorder.md` is a recognised diagnosis
nobody signing fitness-for-duty forms claims to fully understand, and its intake
forms now ask "what did it feel like" before "how afraid were you." That is a
Medical Corps with an admitted epistemic problem, which is a much better place
to put a new doctor than a competent one.

**There is already a doctor.** [Dr. Iona Vale](/star-rangers/characters/dr-iona-vale/)
gates jumps at Threshold Station on central fatigue markers — *if the crew
member shows unresolved central fatigue markers, the jump does not happen* —
and is cited in Sorcha's page. Lekrin needs a relationship to Vale, or a
different posting, or the two will blur.

**Gap worth noting:** there are lore files for the Frontier Corps, Safety Corps
and Science Corps, but **none for the Medical Corps** — it exists only as
references inside other entries. If Lekrin is going to carry it, that file is
the natural companion piece.

**Needs from Dermot:** first name; posting relative to Vale; and whether Lekrin
is on the PTASD side of the Corps (the interesting, unresolved end) or general
practice.
