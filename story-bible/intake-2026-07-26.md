# Intake — 26 July 2026

Raw material from Dermot, captured verbatim before any canon reconciliation, so
nothing is lost while it is being worked through. **Nothing here is canon yet.**
Each block carries a status and the canon checks already run against it.

**Provenance: this is the last of the Sudowrite material** — the ideas worth
keeping out of the parallel draft, with the rest archived. That explains the
shape of the conflicts below. These blocks were written alongside the repository
rather than against it, so they drifted: the Meridian system material describes a
world the repo already gave to Halyx, and the Slipwave material describes a band
the repo deliberately kept small. This is the same two-copies problem the
[Two Copies, Forty Seconds Apart](../src/journal/two-copies-forty-seconds-apart.md)
journal entry is about, arriving one last time.

The rule stands: the repository is the record. Where these blocks disagree with
`src/`, `src/` wins unless Dermot rules otherwise — and where they add something
`src/` does not have, they are worth keeping.

Working order: Fliade (clear) → Umbral Moon (terminology fixes) → Kalypsis Dawn
(naming collisions) → the renamed Meridian system (ruling given, needs new names).

**Open items from this file are indexed in [`open-questions.md`](open-questions.md).**

## 1. Fliade — CLEAR TO WRITE

**Status:** no collisions found. Anstolik and Pandoids are new to the repo.
**One flag:** *"Convergence Point"* is not an established term — it appears
nowhere in `src/` as a defined concept. It needs either a definition of its own
or a mapping onto existing canon (Concordant membranes, boundary zones, or
Lagrange fold points). It recurs in three separate blocks below, so it is worth
settling once.

### Block 1a — physical

> Fliade is a rocky, cold terrestrial planet orbiting at a distance that keeps it well below Earth-like temperatures across most of its surface. The equatorial band is the most hospitable zone, reaching cool temperate conditions at peak season, but even there snowfall is common and standing water freezes for long stretches of the year. The polar regions are permanently glaciated and largely uninhabitable by surface-dwelling life. Most of the planet's biodiversity, including its two dominant species, is concentrated in the vast underground cavern systems that honeycomb the crust. These caverns are warmed by geothermal activity and fed by subterranean rivers, forming self-contained ecosystems that have evolved largely independent of the harsh surface. The Anstolik, giant reindeer-like herbivores, range across both the surface tundra and the upper cavern levels, fiercely defending feeding grounds and migration corridors. The Pandoids, powerfully built humanoid bear-like beings with a calm and reclusive nature, prefer the deeper cavern networks, where their strength and familiarity with the underground terrain give them a natural advantage. Fliade's position in the broader stellar neighborhood has drawn some scientific interest from human space, given that the planet's unusual geothermal activity and its proximity to a suspected Convergence Point may make it relevant to ongoing research into the multiverse's structure.

### Block 1b — sensory

> Fliade greets visitors with biting, thin air and a sky that rarely warms past a pale grey-blue, even at the equator. The sun sits low and distant, casting long shadows across tundra plains dusted with frost even in the warmer months. Wind cuts across open ground with a dry, mineral sharpness, carrying the faint smell of ice and deep stone. Beneath the surface, the cavern networks tell a different story: geothermal warmth seeps through porous rock, and bioluminescent fungi coat the cave walls in soft amber and blue-green light, filling the underground with a dim, steady glow. The air below ground is humid and carries the earthy scent of moss, mineral water, and the warm musk of the creatures that shelter there. Above ground, the crunch of permafrost underfoot and the distant, low bellow of Anstolik herds marking territory are the most constant sounds. Pandoids move quietly through rocky outcroppings and cave mouths, their thick fur blending with the grey stone, their presence announced more by the slow shift of boulders than by any sound they make.

**Open question:** are the Pandoids sapient? "Calm and reclusive nature" reads
that way, but they are listed alongside the Anstolik as one of two "dominant
species", which reads ecological. That distinction decides whether Fliade is a
first-contact world or a wildlife one, and whether Frontier Transformation
Protocols' sapience clause applies.

**ANSWERED — Dermot, 16 August 2026:** *"Yes Pandoids are sapient and one of
very few humanoid like aliens."*

So **Fliade is a first-contact world**, and Frontier Transformation Protocols'
sapience clause applies to it. The Anstolik are unaffected by this ruling and
stay as written — giant reindeer-like herbivores, one of two dominant species in
the ecological sense.

Two things the ruling leaves live, neither blocking:

1. ~~**"Low-tech" is not confirmed**~~ — **#450, ruled 16 August 2026, and the
   question was the wrong shape.** Dermot: *"Technology levels are not directly
   comparable on a simple linear scale."* So *low-tech* is not an answer that
   can be given about the Pandoids or anyone else; there is no scale for it to
   name a point on. What Fliade prose owes instead is **what they actually do** —
   what they make, what they read, what their world never gave them a reason to
   build — with no rank attached and no implied rung missing. The FTP clause
   turns on sapience, which is settled, so nothing about contact was ever
   waiting on this. Drafted into `what-the-record-refuses.md` as a general
   refusal; see the note below.
2. **It touches a published refusal — tracked as #449, and see below.** The
   humanoid half of the ruling collides with canon, and that is a decision
   rather than a detail.

### The humanoid collision — needs a ruling before Fliade is written (#449)

[What the Record Refuses](../src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md) states, in
prose and again in its summary table, that **"the Krenyi are the only other
sapient humanoids"** in this record — and flags that refusal as *"the
load-bearing one"*, the whole point being that this setting is not a humanoid
galaxy. The 13 August species-design work was driven by that ruling; five
species got body plans because three portraits had quietly drifted humanoid.

Sapient, humanoid Pandoids are a second exception to a refusal whose published
wording admits exactly one. Nothing is broken *today* — Fliade is unwritten and
the Pandoids appear nowhere in `src/` — but the first page that describes them
would contradict a live canon page.

**RULED — Dermot, 16 August 2026: reading 3**, with a clarification that does
most of the work: *"Option 3 for the Pandoids is fine, but derived from a bear
like alien ancestor not actual terrestrial bears, despite the name."*

So the Pandoids descend from a **bear-like alien ancestor on Fliade**, not from
anything in Earth's biosphere. *Pandoid* and *bear-like* are the observers'
vocabulary — a first survey reaching for the nearest familiar shape — and they
record what the encounter resembled, never what the lineage is. That is not a
special case: [Cross-Biosphere Taxonomy](../src/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy/)
already holds that life began separately on each world and that nothing in this
record is a metazoan but us. The name was always going to mislead someone; the
ruling is that it misleads about descent and the record says so.

Drafted into `src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md` — the humanoid refusal now
turns on the **human frame** rather than on posture, with the naming caution
stated alongside it. Fliade and the Pandoids are **not named on that page**:
they are unwritten, and a doctrine page is the wrong place for a species to
first enter canon. The distinction stands on its own and the named example can
follow when Fliade is written.

Three readings, the third now taken:

- **They are not humanoid in the refusal's sense.** The refusal targets people
  "with two arms, two legs and one head, differing from humans in colour, ridge
  and ear" — the rubber-forehead alien. A powerfully built ursine being is
  bipedal without being a human with a nose job, and Dermot's own phrasing was
  *humanoid **like***. On this reading the refusal stands untouched and Fliade
  prose simply writes them as bear-derived rather than man-shaped.
- **The refusal widens to two named exceptions.** Krenyi and Pandoids, both
  remarked on rather than typical. This is a canon change to a load-bearing
  published page and needs a PR he approves, plus a check of the summary table
  row and the 13 August species-design premise.
- **The tiers are distinct** — human-frame humanoid (Krenyi) versus
  bipedal-but-not-human-derived (Pandoids) — and the refusal gains a sentence
  distinguishing them. This is the *explain the complexity in-story* option: the
  distinction is more interesting than either flat answer, and the record has
  reason to draw it precisely.

## 2. The Umbral Moon — WRITABLE AFTER THREE FIXES

**Terminology fixes needed before this can go in:**

1. **"Ethereal" → "Etheric."** Canon uses Etheric in 59 files; Ethereal appears
   in exactly one, almost certainly a slip.
2. **"Shadow Universe" has no canonical existence** — but
   [Sí Gaoithe: The Discordant Membrane](../src/lore/universes/si-gaoithe.md) is
   close enough to be worth checking first. It is already tagged `gravity-tunnel`
   and `meta-dimensional`, described as bleeding across boundary zones at
   unpredictable intervals, high creative-entropy, and standing as the working
   explanation for an old body of folklore. The shadow beings may already have a
   home. Named realms in canon are Sí Gaoithe, Tír Tairngire (the Harmonic
   Membrane) and Threnos-Ω.
3. **"magic" breaks a hard rule.** Stated flatly and repeatedly: *"No physics in
   this universe is supernatural… The difference from the real cosmos is not
   magic. It is architecture."* The effect described is fine; it has to be
   expressed as lawful cross-layer coupling through the Five Layers.

**Two smaller conflicts:**

- **The Kingdom of the Four Islands is a planet**, in an unnamed system, settled
  by a human departure older than any Solar System record — not a place on
  Earth's surface.
- **Eden Space Habitat** is a Class I Fold-Proximate Research Platform; confirm
  it is in Low Earth Orbit before writing that.

**What works well:** placing the fold at Earth–Moon L5 puts it exactly where New
London holds station, and the material already has New London logging the
disturbances. That is a good, load-bearing connection.

### Block 2a — structure and position

> The Umbral Moon occupies a stable position within a higher-dimensional fold that creases the boundary between the Material Universe and the Shadow Universe. Unlike a wormhole, this fold does not connect two distant points in space. Instead, it bends the geometry of both universes inward along a shared axis, forming a crease where the two realms press against each other. Running through that crease is a gravity tunnel, a channel of concentrated gravitational force generated by the competing pulls of both universes, and the moon sits suspended within it, held in place rather than drifting fully into either realm. The fold sits in near-Earth space along the Earth-Moon L5 corridor, placing it within gravitational and dimensional reach of Eden Space Habitat in Low Earth Orbit and the Kingdom of the Four Islands on Earth's surface. Its position within the fold shifts slightly over time as the fold breathes, but the gravity tunnel keeps it from escaping the crease entirely. Because the moon straddles the boundary, shadow beings and other entities from the Shadow Universe can cross into the Material Universe here more easily than at any other known point in the solar system. New London Space Habitat, stationed at Earth-Moon L5, has been logging the Ethereal disturbances radiating outward from the fold's edges, though the moon itself remained undetected until Eden's AI research systems flagged the anomalous gravitational micro-pulses.
>
> The Umbral Moon is home to elusive shadow beings, which are entities native to the Shadow Universe. These beings possess a fluid, mutable form that shifts with the ambient energy patterns of their realm. While they lack a fixed physical structure, they manifest as dark silhouettes with glowing eyes of cold green or pale silver. On rare occasions, more substantial creatures cross into the Material Universe through the fold, appearing as ghostly apparitions that can manipulate shadows and subtly influence the environment around them. These shadow beings are drawn to the disturbances caused by magic and often interact with the inhabitants of Eden Space Habitat, either out of curiosity or with more enigmatic intentions.

### Block 2b — sensory

> The Umbral Moon is nearly impossible to see from Earth's surface or from Eden Space Habitat without sensitive instruments. Its crust absorbs incoming light rather than reflecting it, a property researchers attribute to the dimensional fold pressing against the rock and pulling photons partially into the Shadow Universe before they can bounce back. Through deep-field sensors, it appears as a dark smear against the star field, its edges occasionally flickering with the pale, shifting colors characteristic of the Shadow Universe: deep violets, cold greens, and sudden washes of silver that pulse and fade like a slow breath. Observers in pressurized vessels stationed nearby report a low, bone-deep hum that standard instruments cannot fully capture, as though the gravity tunnel running through the fold vibrates at a frequency just below the threshold of human hearing. The air inside those vessels carries a faint metallic taste, and shadows fall at wrong angles, pooling in corners where the light should reach. When the fold is active, the moon's silhouette shimmers and briefly doubles, as if a second version of it exists a half-step out of phase with the material world. Shadow beings crossing through the fold leave cold pressure waves that sensors read as gravitational micro-pulses, distinct from normal tidal forces.

## 3. The Kalypsis Dawn — WRITABLE AFTER TWO NAMING DECISIONS

**Fits canon well.** An unarmed, endurance-built, neutral-passage vessel is
exactly what the charter's unconditional neutrality and humanitarian mandate
imply, and the reasoning given — arming it would void the neutral-passage
agreements — is the charter's own logic. Tissadelle Shepherd as Line Captain
matches her Season 5 rank. **"Overfold" is already established canon** (9 files).

**Two naming collisions:**

1. **"Warden-Class"** collides with **[The Eden Warden](../src/characters/eden-warden.md)**,
   an existing Kernel-compliant plural AI collective — and the Kalypsis Dawn is
   stationed in the fold-adjacent corridor near Eden. Same region, same word.
   Either rename the class or make the echo deliberate.
2. **"Star Ranger Corps"** appears in **0** files. The organisation is *the Star
   Rangers*; "Corps" is reserved for its subdivisions — Survey, Frontier, Safety,
   Science, Engineering. Should read "the Star Rangers" throughout.

**New terms with no canon entry yet** — all fine to coin, but they need
definitions somewhere: `shadow-matter`, `flux-shear`, `beacon-array`,
`beacon harmonics`, `Tier-3 Overfold Rating`, `Cognitive-Bridge AI`.

**One to check:** Helion-3 is described as a "non-autonomous Cognitive-Bridge
AI". Canon regulates AI tightly through the [AI Safety Kernel](../src/lore/ai-safety-kernel.md)
and Kernel-compliance shows up on other AI entities. Helion-3 should probably
carry an explicit compliance status.

### Block 3a — the ship

> The Kalypsis Dawn is a Warden-Class Frigate operated by the Star Ranger Corps, designated SR-WF-17 and currently under the command of Line Captain Tissadelle Shepherd. At 142 meters in length, the ship is built for endurance and stability rather than speed or firepower. Its hull incorporates shadow-matter shielding, hull resonance dampers, and a flux-stabilizer core that together give it a Tier-3 Overfold Rating, one of the highest stability certifications available to a non-military vessel. The ship carries no offensive weapons. Its defensive suite consists of flux-shear deflectors, an EM-scatter veil, and Overfold turbulence shields, all rated for environmental hazards rather than combat.
>
> What sets the Kalypsis Dawn apart from most frigates is its modular interior, designed from the keel up to support radically different biologies. The habitat section includes a pressurized Aquatic Corridor with holo-interfaces, a low-gravity Gas-Giant Floater Chamber with electromagnetic scaffolding, a vibration-stable Crystalline Resonance Bay fitted with harmonic communication nodes, flux-dampened cabins for Overfold-sensitive crew, and a Distributed-Mind Grid for non-localized species. The medical deck carries a multispecies trauma ward, a xenobiology lab, a shadow-matter exposure chamber, and emergency stasis pods. The cargo and courier bay holds a secure diplomatic vault, scientific sample containment, humanitarian supply racks, and rapid-deployment evacuation pods.
>
> The bridge is organized around a panoramic holo-display, an Overfold drift predictor, a diplomatic channel suite, and the interface for Helion-3, the ship's non-autonomous Cognitive-Bridge AI, which handles interspecies communication and real-time hazard modeling. Command chairs are flux-stabilized to keep the crew functional during turbulence spikes. The engineering section houses a beacon-array diagnostic cradle and a complement of modular repair drones.
>
> The ship's Overfold systems include Tri-Axis Flux Stabilizers that reduce turbulence impact by roughly forty percent, a Beacon-Link Navigation Suite that reads beacon harmonics in real time, shadow-matter buffering that protects the crew from decoherence waves, and an Emergency Node-Hop Protocol allowing short-range Overfold jumps if a corridor collapses mid-transit. Operational range without resupply is eighteen months, and the ship is rated for a crew of forty to sixty multispecies personnel.

### Block 3b — the class and its history

> The Warden-Class was developed by the Star Ranger Corps as a direct response to the expansion of Overfold corridor networks and the growing number of species requiring neutral, safe passage through contested or unstable regions. Earlier Corps vessels were adapted from civilian transports or decommissioned courier ships, and they lacked the structural integrity to survive sustained turbulence near fold-adjacent space. The loss of several such vessels during the early corridor-mapping era prompted the Corps to commission a purpose-built platform.
>
> The design brief was deliberately non-military. The Corps had negotiated neutral-passage agreements with dozens of factions, alliances, and independent systems, and those agreements depended on the Corps maintaining a credible claim to non-aggression. Arming the new class would have voided most of those agreements. Engineers instead focused on passive resilience: shadow-matter shielding, flux stabilization, and the modular habitat architecture that would allow the ship to carry refugees, delegates, or scientific teams without modification between missions.
>
> The Kalypsis Dawn is the seventeenth hull of the class. It was commissioned after the Corps identified a need for a dedicated diplomatic and hazard-response vessel in the fold-adjacent corridor near Eden Space Habitat and the Umbral Moon region. Its assignment to Line Captain Tissadelle Shepherd followed her record of maintaining crew cohesion during high-turbulence operations aboard an earlier, less capable vessel. The ship has since accumulated a notable operational history, including the Vespera-9 Mediation Run, in which Shepherd guided five-faction delegates through a turbulence corridor during a beacon-array flicker; the Shardfall Evacuation, in which the ship transported three thousand civilians including crystalline and aquatic refugees from a collapsing mining outpost; and Operation Luminous Drift, a hazard-mapping mission in the historic Drift region during which the Kalypsis Dawn stabilized a convoy through a turbulence spike.

### Block 4a — system and phenomena

> The Meridian star system is visually dominated by its spectacular electromagnetic phenomena. The star itself is a stable K-type orange dwarf, casting a warm amber glow across its planets. Meridian Prime appears as a blue-green marble with swirling cloud patterns and three small moons, while The Sentinel is a breathtaking gas giant with luminescent rings that pulse with electromagnetic activity in blues, purples, and greens. These rings emit a constant low-frequency hum that can be detected by specialized equipment, occasionally forming complex patterns that scientists believe constitute a form of communication.
>
> The habitable zones of Meridian Prime feature diverse biomes dominated by spiral-shaped flora that grow in mathematical patterns, suggesting evolutionary adaptation to the system's unique electromagnetic fields. The indigenous sentient species, called the Spiralites, are semi-translucent beings with bioluminescent neural networks visible beneath their skin. They communicate through subtle electromagnetic pulses and build intricate spiral-shaped habitats that resonate with The Sentinel's ring frequencies.
>
> Human settlements on Meridian Prime are characterized by utilitarian architecture with extensive electromagnetic shielding, as the planet's natural fields interfere with certain technologies. The colony's mining operations and agricultural expansion emit disruptive frequencies that are slowly destroying the delicate electromagnetic harmony that the Spiralites depend on for communication and reproduction.

### Block 4b — position and cosmology

> The Meridian star system exists approximately 700 light years from Earth's Solar System in the Material Universe, positioned near what scientists believe may be a minor Convergence Point where the Material and Ethereal Universes experience subtle overlap. This positioning explains the unusual electromagnetic properties of the system and particularly The Sentinel's apparent sentience.
>
> The system consists of six planets total, with Meridian Prime being the third from the star and the only traditionally habitable world. The Sentinel is the fifth planet, a massive gas giant with its complex ring system. The human colony on Meridian Prime was established approximately 30 years ago through a joint venture between Military Space Command and Wayland Space Corporation, who detected unusual energy signatures suggesting valuable resources.
>
> What makes the Meridian system unique is that it appears to exist simultaneously in both the Material and Ethereal Universes, creating natural conditions for consciousness to develop in unexpected forms. The Sentinel's distress signal manifests as both conventional electromagnetic transmissions and as emotional impressions that sensitive individuals experience as dreams or visions, suggesting it may be utilizing principles similar to Convergence Weaving to communicate across vast distances.

### Block 5a — the class

> The Horizon-Class Explorer is a 178-meter deep-range survey vessel operated by the Deep Space Rangers, built for endurance, scientific rigor, and multispecies habitability rather than speed or combat. Its hull is layered with resonance-damping composites and long-duration flux shielding, allowing the crew to remain safely aboard during extended exposure to Overfold turbulence that would cripple a lighter vessel. Its Overfold Rating of Tier-4 (Extreme Stability) is the highest in routine fleet service.

### Block 5b — history and the Silver Meridian

> The Horizon-Class emerged from hard lessons learned during the early expansion era, when lighter survey ships were lost to Overfold drift events they lacked the sensors to predict and the shielding to survive. The Deep Space Rangers commissioned the class specifically to fill the gap between fast courier vessels and heavy military platforms, producing a ship that could go where no one had mapped and stay long enough to actually understand what it found.
>
> The Silver Meridian, hull designation SR-HX-04, is one of the most decorated ships in the class. Its Shattered Beacon Survey recovered fragments of an Imperium-era navigation array whose design principles directly informed modern flux-stabilizer engineering, a contribution that shortened subsequent survey missions across the Outer Drift Regions by a measurable margin. The Whispering Node Expedition mapped a drifting Overfold node emitting non-linguistic harmonic signals; the breakthrough in decoding the pattern came from Line Captain Aru-Ten, whose tri-body cognition allowed simultaneous processing of signal threads that a single-mind analyst would have had to sequence one at a time. Most recently, the Luminous Veil Encounter brought the crew into first contact with a species that communicates entirely through Overfold resonance. Starwarden Lysa Varrin's patient, non-symbolic approach to the exchange prevented a misunderstanding that could have destabilized the surrounding region.

## 6. Slipwave — RULING GIVEN: reconcile via option (c)

> rooms, under two years, no studio album, never reformed, Shepherd never
> rejoining. The Drift Engine, the multispecies translation work, the

>

**This is a Meridian-scale conflict.** `glossary/slipwave.md` is detailed, and

- **(b)** Retcon Slipwave upward. Costs the unglamorous dock-circuit framing that

  already has them drifting toward polish: *Protectors of the Fold* is described
  as "mixing-booth-polished", and they have become "recognisable" without
  meaning to. The grand version becomes what those two built over decades, with
  Shepherd's absence intact.

### Block 6a — mission

> Slipwave's core mission was always to make music that crossed species lines without flattening what made each species distinct. Rather than translating songs into a lowest-common-denominator format, the band worked with xenolinguist consultants and alien interpreter-musicians to find structural equivalents: the harmonic intervals a Crystalline listener experiences as emotion, the sub-bass pulse a gas-dweller reads as rhythm, the light-flicker cadence that carries melody for photosensitive species. Their AI composition suite, nicknamed the Drift Engine by the band, generated real-time adaptive arrangements so that a single live performance could be heard as a coherent, culturally resonant piece by dozens of species simultaneously. Beyond the music itself, Slipwave pioneered low-gravity shuffle dance as a performance art form, developing choreography that used microgravity drift, magnetic-sole footwork, and tether-assisted spins to create movement vocabulary impossible in full gravity. They documented and published their translation methodology openly, hoping other artists would build on it. In that sense the band saw itself as a proof of concept: that culture could travel across species boundaries without requiring anyone to give up their own perceptual world.

### Block 6b — origins

> Slipwave emerged aboard Eden Space Habitat, likely within the Residential Rings or the Garden Core, areas known for their vibrant multispecies communities. The founding members, young humans rooted in Celtic musical traditions, were captivated by the tonal intricacies and oral storytelling of their heritage, using it as a foundation for their unique sound. Among these pioneers was Tissadelle Shepherd, whose later acclaim for calm precision and cross-cultural diplomacy in the Star Rangers Corps can be traced back to her time with Slipwave. Here, she honed the ability to navigate diverse audiences, foster common ground without compromising individuality, and maintain composure amid communicative challenges. The band quickly gained prominence on the habitat circuit, captivating multispecies audiences at cultural events and eventually embarking on tours across other orbital installations, including New London Space Habitat. Though initially met with skepticism by the neo-Victorian audiences there, the Drift Engine's adaptive capabilities soon won them over. Slipwave's initial journey concluded when Tissadelle departed to join the Star Rangers Corps, leaving the band's future uncertain. However, after about ten years, Slipwave reformed and became the most famous interstellar music show in the galaxy. Their innovative translation methodologies endured as a niche academic reference in xenolinguistics, while their low-gravity choreography continues to influence habitat performance training programs.

### Block 6c — base of operations

> Slipwave had no fixed headquarters in the traditional sense. As a touring band built for habitat performance spaces, they operated out of wherever they were booked. Their home base was Eden Space Habitat, where they rehearsed in the lower Residential Rings in compartments repurposed as sound-isolated studio space. The Garden Core biodome occasionally served as a venue for open performances, with its variable-gravity zones making it ideal for low-gravity shuffle choreography rehearsals. Their technical equipment, including the Drift Engine AI rig and the modular instrument arrays used by their alien interpreter-musicians, was stored in a shared cargo bay registered to the band on Eden Station.
